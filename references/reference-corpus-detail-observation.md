# Reference-Corpus Distinctive-Detail Observation Guide

This guide supplements `distinctive-detail-specification.md`. It was derived from a review of 109 retained reference-image files. Near-duplicate grouping produced 73 visual representatives for qualitative inspection. The study records reusable observation criteria. Source-backed corpora may also carry a native-dimension source-derived perceptual vector projection; that derived artifact remains under the rights governing the source and is not reusable preset knowledge. For whole-image line, proportion, camera, gaze, light, surface, support, depth, and staging analysis, use `reference-corpus-visual-technique-observation.md` before classifying a preset.

## Evidence discipline

1. Separate **stable identity detail** from **expression-dependent structure**, **scene-transient effects**, and **style artifacts**. Sweat, blush, water droplets, rim light, shadow edges, motion lines, and compression noise remain scene or rendering information. Only an explicit approved Identity, Era, Form, or Appearance Contract may assign an observed property to a durable owner.
2. Use subject-relative laterality. A cropped or hidden opposite side remains unknown.
3. Prefer a stable anatomical or material anchor, then use a local proportion. Pixel dimensions are unsuitable for series continuity.
4. Treat a single clear view as sufficient for a visible-side record, while opposite-side symmetry requires direct evidence or user confirmation.
5. Normalize across style: confirm that a line interrupts surface structure, repeats across views, or follows a physical landmark before classifying it as a scar, wrinkle, tattoo, chip, or seam.


## Source-artifact cleanup

Discard clearly non-diegetic censor masks, opaque editorial blocks, mosaics, blur patches, stickers, bars, replacement shapes, watermarks, signatures, logos, UI remnants, and meaningless source text before measuring distinctive details. Do not record the artifact or the fact of concealment as anatomy, a body marking, a scar, a missing part, an occlusion condition, or a reusable prompt feature. Continue from visible landmarks and from the selected subject contract. Exact geometry requires supporting evidence from the contract or another source.

## Feature-family criteria

### Eyes
Record eye-opening shape, iris diameter relative to eye width, pupil geometry and axis, sclera exposure, limbal-ring thickness, opacity, laterality, and catchlight behavior separately. Eyelid angle and pupil dilation may be expression or lighting dependent; iris hue, clouding, heterochromia, and stable pupil class are identity candidates.

Useful anchors: eye width, iris diameter, inner and outer eye corners, upper and lower lid arcs, brow-to-eye spacing.

### Nose and muzzle
Record nose planform, width relative to muzzle, projection, vertical thickness, bridge transition, nostril count and spacing, philtrum connection, material response, and centerline alignment. Record muzzle reach and muzzle depth separately. When perspective, markings, or fur obscure the outer silhouette, also record the cast-shadow footprint beneath the nose, lip line, and lower muzzle, plus the side-plane shadow along the cheek-to-muzzle transition. Distinguish a permanent pale nose patch from a highlight, a flatter thin feline nose plate on a short deep blunt muzzle from a thicker projecting canine nose pad on a longer tapering muzzle, and a lighting edge from a stable anatomical boundary.

Useful anchors: eye width, muzzle width, muzzle height, brow-stop to nose distance, nose width, nose thickness, nostril spacing, mouth-center line, and lower-muzzle shadow length.

### Ears
Record base attachment, ear height relative to head, base width, rotation, fold position, inner-ear field, rim thickness, tip geometry, tuft distribution, and any notch or missing segment. Large-feline defaults favor rounded or softly squared tips with substantial base width, while wolf-like and upright-eared canine defaults favor pointed tips and a taller triangular outline. Ear shape supports a family diagnosis but stays subordinate to user or contract species authority and to correlated muzzle and nose evidence. Ear rotation may express mood; fold position, tip damage, and stable inner coloration can define identity. For species diagnosis, treat rounded large-feline tips and pointed canine tips as supporting evidence. Stylization can sharpen a feline ear or round a canine silhouette, so combine ear evidence with muzzle reach, shadow footprint, nose projection, cheek volume, and jaw depth.

Useful anchors: head height, outer-eye line, ear base, ear midpoint, ear tip, inner-field boundary.

### Cheeks, ruffs, and muzzle follicles
Record the origin and end of cheek wedges, clump count, flow direction, projection relative to muzzle length, color grouping, and silhouette contribution. Whisker-dot clusters use bounded counts, local arcs, and muzzle-pad anchors.

Useful anchors: outer eye corner, muzzle corner, jaw angle, ear base, neck-ruff boundary.

### Claws, nails, pads, and knuckles
Record the exact digit, visible digit count, exposure state, projection relative to terminal digit length, curvature, cross-section, color, gloss, chip or missing segment, pad map, and callus placement. A perspective-shortened claw still belongs to a specific digit.

Useful anchors: terminal digit length, palm or paw width, knuckle width, pad diameter, claw base.

### Scars, burns, and missing material
Record start and end landmarks, line width and length separately, path, depth evidence, healed state, color, edge softness, and how fur, skin, scales, or casing resume around the feature. A shadow edge or coat marking remains separate unless physical surface interruption is visible.

Useful anchors: eye height, chest width, shoulder cap, forearm length, scale row, panel seam.

### Tattoos and pigment marks
Record motif class, coverage, line weight, color, opacity, orientation, curvature, anatomical anchor, and whether fur or hair modifies visibility. Preserve original fictional geometry; do not retain signatures, readable logos, or franchise identifiers from references.

Useful anchors: forearm band, shoulder cap, chest centerline, scapular region, thigh panel, tail circumference.

### Wrinkles and folds
Record count, spacing, orientation, depth, taper, and activation condition. Separate permanent age or fatigue folds from temporary brow compression, smile creases, fabric wrinkles, fur separations, and painted hatching.

Useful anchors: inter-brow distance, lower-lid arc, mouth corner, nasolabial path, joint crease.

### Teeth, horns, antlers, and scales
Record count, base attachment, growth axis, tip shape, branching order, break or crack location, surface rings, color, and cross-view overlap. For scales, record row direction, cell size, patch boundary, and transition back to intact rows.

## Confidence and promotion

- **High**: clearly visible with stable anchors or repeated across views. Eligible for signature or supporting lock.
- **Medium**: visible in one useful view with partial occlusion or stylization. Preserve the concept and allow geometry adaptation.
- **Low**: inferred from compression, lighting, crop, or hidden continuation. Keep as an observation note and ask before locking.

Promote a corpus observation into a reusable preset only when the construction generalizes beyond one character, can be described affirmatively, has at least three invariants and three nearby misreadings, and does not preserve source-specific text, logos, signatures, or copyrighted character identity.
## Camera, garment, hair, and terminal-detail extraction

When a supplied image is used as preset evidence, record camera and coverage as measured relationships rather than broad shot labels. Capture shot scale, subject-relative distance, camera height, angle, lens consequence, frame occupancy, exact top and bottom body landmarks, side crop anchors, nearest form, and mandatory visible elements.

Trace each garment as connected panels and openings. Record neckline, hood or collar attachment, shoulder support, sleeve or armhole geometry, side-panel width, front and rear panel extent, hem, waistband, rise, inseam, outseam, leg opening, coverage, appendage clearance, pose response, support-surface displacement, and tension paths.

For each actual observed or explicitly authored structure, record the relevant location, boundary, dimensions, material, color and uncertainty. Use author-owned IDs rather than a universal anatomical checklist. No head-hair, brow, lash, digit, claw or other named example is mandatory. Hidden is not absent; uncertainty is not permission to invent. Write detailed observations as geometry properties of that structure. See [Declared Structures Runtime](runtime/declared-structures.md).
