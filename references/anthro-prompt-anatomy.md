# Anthro Prompt Anatomy

Use this knowledge for a construction question selected through
[Production direction](runtime/production-direction.md). The intended act,
state or relationship remains the unit of the depiction. Species, body,
surface and garment descriptions help resolve a required design distinction
or an observed failure within it. An intelligible action or appearance can
carry its ordinary construction without a separate instruction for every part.

## Species and declared structure

When identity or body construction needs clarification, describe the declared
features that distinguish the design. A local design description can connect
species, muzzle, ears, limbs and tail as follows:

```text
anthropomorphic black wolf; upright triangular ears; long structured canine muzzle;
large paw-like hands with dark claws; powerful canine lower legs; one large coherent tail
```

For a hybrid whose regional construction matters, identify which lineage
applies to which region:

```text
wolf head and tail, broad feline torso markings, digitigrade canine legs
```

## Body and proportion

When `muscular` leaves an important proportion unresolved, available structural
distinctions include:

```text
massive broad shoulders, thick trapezius, deep chest, narrow waist,
large forearms, powerful thighs, stable head-to-body ratio
```

When the desired design is cute, childlike, chibi, mature, elderly, or otherwise age-coded, state it as a normal proportion and facial-design choice. Do not add such a descriptor when it was not requested.

Use the distinction the depiction needs. The list does not turn a body-build
choice into separate obligations to expose every muscle or body region.

## Surface construction

### Short fur

```text
short close-lying matte fur grouped into broad color planes,
sparse tufts at the cheeks, neck ruff, elbows, calves, and outer silhouette
```

### Long fur

Mass and direction explain how long fur responds to the current pose:

```text
layered long fur forming a heavy neck mantle and forearm tufts,
large coherent clumps following gravity and body movement
```

### Scales, feathers, and plates

Use species-appropriate terms instead of automatically adding fur:

```text
small matte scales with larger throat plates
layered contour feathers with broad wing-cover shapes
segmented chitin plates with restrained highlights
```

## Coat and markings

Location, laterality and symmetry distinguish markings when their design is
required. The following are possible design statements:

```text
charcoal base coat; lighter muzzle, throat, chest, and inner forearms;
three faded diagonal scars on the character's right upper arm;
identical amber eye design in every image
```

## Wardrobe and material separation

Construction and material relationships can resolve a garment that is unclear
in the selected action or view:

```text
matte black leather vest with a continuous front opening, silver studs,
one chest chain attached between two rings, fitted dark woven shirt,
dark cargo pants with one thigh pocket, heavy lace-up combat boots
```

For near-black outfits, separate materials by value and highlight behavior so fur, leather, cotton, and metal do not merge.

## Action and contact

Begin with the act and its participants. If a required grip, attachment or
overlap remains unresolved, paths and endpoints can establish that connection:

```text
The left forearm rises in front of the torso and the left hand pinches the chain's top link.
The chain hangs freely in one continuous curve without wrapping around the tail.
The right hand rests beside the belt buckle.
```

The complete act may already establish these relations. Use local wording
where it chooses a meaningful connection, rather than decomposing every action
into limb positions.

## Camera and coverage

For a selected frame whose crop or perspective needs definition, the available
descriptions include:

```text
full-body three-quarter view, eye level slightly below the chest,
moderate low-angle emphasis, controlled perspective, both boots fully visible
```

Select coverage for the artifact's purpose. Preserving an identity or garment
does not independently require a view of all its parts.

## Rendering behavior

Line, color, shading and surface behavior can specify a finish more precisely
than its label alone when that distinction matters:

```text
crisp tapered linework, bold outer contour, thinner interior anatomy lines,
flat matte local colors, two-step hard-edged cel shading,
controlled soft highlights, simplified fur planes, minimal background texture
```

## Structural and image boundaries

A declared design or required frame can need concrete counts, continuity or
image-integrity boundaries. Examples of that vocabulary are:

```text
exactly two arms and two hands; exactly one tail; one continuous chain;
complete boots and soles inside frame; readable garment seams;
no accidental text, watermark, or signature
```

The current request and declared structures determine which boundaries apply.
They do not follow automatically from anthropomorphic subjecthood.

## Frame and surface character

When muscularity is being mistaken for skeletal angularity, distinguish frame
character from body build. A thick-boned subject can retain a smooth or rounded
surface while its muscles remain large.

Example diagnostic pattern for a thick-boned smooth or rounded subject:

```text
Under the outer surface, the frame is thick-boned and the visible masses stay smooth and rounded. The cheek forms one full convex volume from the eye line to the jaw, the cheekbone landmark remains submerged, the broad jaw turns through a soft rounded corner, and the joints and muscle handoffs use the same rounded structural language. Muscularity does not introduce chiseled facial planes or hollow cheeks.
```

This is a candidate structural pattern, not a universal wording requirement. Adapt it to the declared species, surface system, and style family.

## Relative measures and accessory construction

Use relative measures when a particular proportion, attachment or repeated
element must reconstruct consistently, or when an observed mismatch makes that
relationship the question. Examples include muzzle length against head depth,
earring diameter against ear height, tag width against nose width, chain unit
against tag width, and buckle span against palm width. Count, side, support,
layer order and pose response can supply the required distinction. Carry that
distinction into the authored depiction; leave unneeded measurements in the
design or reference record.
