# Visual construction and finishing

## 1. Purpose

This reference supplies craft knowledge for turning an intended experience into
a particular picture. It connects the scale and movement of the whole image to
the depiction of bodies, objects, marks, color, light, lettering and information.
These decisions can originate a conception; they are not a cosmetic pass after
the scene has determined the picture.

Use it when presentation or treatment remains open, when a reference contributes
a visual construction, or when the right subject keeps appearing in the wrong
kind of image. An informal wish for an impressive design, an intimate portrait
or a personal world can raise this question without naming a technique. Named
media, photographic looks, 3D or toy presentation, type, logos, several regions
and environment-led work also activate the relevant knowledge here.

[Production direction](runtime/production-direction.md) owns interpretation,
source use, dialogue, conception and revision. [Creative core](creative-core.md)
connects purpose to the choice of construction. The examples below supply
specific ways to build a picture and explain what changes their effect. They
are authored possibilities, not reports of generated results, defaults for a
subject or a set of forms that every commission must use.

## 2. The five finishing layers

These layers locate the knowledge needed to realize a chosen relationship.
They do not independently choose the picture, and need not all be authored.
A fixed choice remains an anchor at its declared scope. Flat graphics and
lettering can be complete without a physical camera or lighting setup.

1. Medium grammar describes the marks and surfaces of a treatment: flat cel, watercolor, photograph, 3D render, clay, pixel art, or another authored treatment. Apply it at its selected scope through the [render intent](runtime/render-contract.md). Mixed treatments need defined relationships and boundaries, including deliberate transitions or material combinations within one subject.
2. Optical system is how the frame sees. It covers focal behavior (telephoto compression, wide-angle stretch, fisheye), focus falloff (depth of field, blurry foreground or background), exposure character (overexposure, film grain, halation, chromatic aberration), and lens-era cues (film stock looks, analog artifacts).
3. Light architecture is the physical arrangement: source count, direction, size, and color; named setups when photographic (three-point, Rembrandt, butterfly, split, rim); environment behavior when scenic (golden hour, blue hour, overcast, volumetric fog, crepuscular rays). Light architecture is physical; medium grammar determines how it is drawn. Designed value shapes can also organize a graphic without implying a physical lighting rig.
4. Material response is how surfaces answer the light within the chosen medium: PBR separation for 3D, glistening or wet highlights, subsurface warmth, metallic versus matte versus translucent, toy-surface smoothness, fabric weave. A material term that contradicts the medium, such as photoreal skin pores on a vinyl figure, is a layer conflict to resolve rather than to stack.
5. Presentation finish realizes the construction selected in Production direction. It covers the treatment of backgrounds, borders, lettering, diagrams and other components, plus relevant post effects. A form's familiar furniture contributes only where it serves the content and hierarchy.

## 3. Composition order

Develop content, presentation and treatment together. A material, a silhouette,
a printed surface, a view or a light behavior can originate an idea. Settle the
relationships on which other choices depend. Treat bodies, words, objects and
fields as participants in the same construction. Their roles determine their
scale, location and crossings; a completed figure does not reserve its footprint
before the rest of the picture is composed.

When letters are the place a body occupies, usable glyph shapes and the seated
pose must support each other. When the image is about a tiny reflection on a
tool, its surface, the action and the viewing direction develop together. When
a garment carries print, the graphic and the major folds determine the shared
pattern. When a mechanical extension defines a wearer's new silhouette, its
mass and the body's openings organize the small connections. Develop each
dependency at the scale that changes the whole picture, returning between the
whole and its local contacts as necessary. These are relationships to resolve,
not a sequence of independently completed parts or rendering passes.

Use the distinction between **depicted space** and the **picture surface**.
Depicted space locates a body, a chair, a wall, smoke or light in a world. The
picture surface organizes the visible shapes on the page or screen. A sleeve
can simultaneously be cloth around an arm and a dark diagonal that carries the
composition. A word can be readable language, a large flat shape and an apparent
support for a body. State the consequential relationship when these roles meet.

| Construction | What establishes the relationship | What changing it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A scene viewed through a frame | Shared scale, perspective, support, light and occlusion connect its contents | Giving every object its own frontal display view turns inhabitable space into an inventory |
| Images and type arranged on a page | Alignment, intervals, overlapping silhouettes, repeated color or marks connect otherwise separate contents | Adding a literal room behind them can replace the designed page with a scene |
| A body occupying a flat graphic | Selected contacts, crossings and scale make the graphic act as a place while its surface remains flat | Extruding every letter or shape can remove the tension between drawing and physical support |
| A scene breaking a panel boundary | The frame establishes an inside; selected parts continue into the surrounding page | Removing the frame removes the event of crossing it, even if the same vehicle and smoke remain |
| Several treatments of one subject | Identity or design stays shared while each region has a distinct expressive or informational job | Uniform treatment can erase the difference between an intimate face, a lived scene and an emblem |

### Visibility and surface order

Compose what covers what as part of the large shape pattern. Subject importance,
attention and front-to-back order can agree or differ. An important figure can
sit behind a broad foreground word; a large rear word can outweigh a small
unobstructed figure; a near shoulder can conceal the person the scene is about.
Select the relationship for the intended pressure, depth, intimacy, disruption
or information. There is no fixed character, decoration and background stack.

Distinguish the kinds of crossing that the picture actually uses:

| Crossing | Visible construction | Effect on the whole |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A nearby object in depicted space | Its silhouette interrupts the body at the corresponding depth; contact, perspective and light explain the relation | The viewer encounters the scene from within it, with information withheld by that position |
| An opaque mark on the picture surface | A word, band, image fragment or painted mass continues across body and field and replaces their pixels where it crosses | Separate contents share a graphic rhythm; the figure is encountered through an edited surface |
| An outline, translucent print or cutout | The marked edge, transmitted color or aperture determines exactly which underlying regions continue to show | Two forms can be read together with a selected density and degree of interference |
| Alternating local crossings | A contour disappears at one intersection and reappears over another, with the selected spatial or graphic logic | The forms interlock; a single global front/back assignment cannot describe them |

Choose the extent and boundaries of the crossing. A tiny accent touching a
sleeve creates a different force from a word spanning the torso, a broad strip
crossing a cheek, or a near mass concealing most of the body. A title can cover
hair, a face or clothing when that is the chosen construction. Its meaningful
strokes may themselves disappear behind another form. Whether the viewer must
recover the entire word, a particular expression or a product detail comes from
the commission and the selected effect. A drawing can remain structurally
coherent while substantial portions are inferred.

Opacity and edges are constructive choices. Converting an intended solid band
into an outline, routing it around a face, making it translucent or moving it
behind the body changes the picture. Those can be useful new arrangements;
they do not preserve the force of the original crossing automatically. Likewise,
adding foreground clutter to a quiet image with rear lettering can destroy its
space. Preserve or author the particular relationship and degree that the
whole work needs.

For a forceful graphic portrait, one possible construction lets a dark sleeve,
a rotated solid word and a short colored cut form one broken diagonal across
the page. The word covers part of the sleeve and cheek; a hair contour crosses
another letter; the exposed eye and jaw remain fragments within that combined
shape pattern. Differences of fill, outline and drawn texture make the viewer
alternate between reading the word and assembling the person. Giving the face
a clear window and moving the word beside it would restore a conventional
portrait but remove that pressure. Another commission may want that open face;
choose it for its effect rather than treating it as the default repair.

Investigate unresolved construction or wording under
[Prompt-only Core](runtime/prompt-only-core.md#knowledge-and-sources). Supplied
material and complete knowledge already read can resolve a choice. The
dictionary carries typography, landscape, photography, illustration, rendering,
materials and toy-finish terminology; catalog modules supply recurring craft
relationships. Inspect the complete selected knowledge at its useful scope.
There is no lookup per attribute or requirement to use both systems.

The selected target's `prompt_input` separately governs rendition. A `tag-based`
input requires real [dictionary search and vocabulary selection](runtime/prompt-vocabulary.md#select-wording-for-a-tag-based-model),
including when the construction is already understood. Record actual
investigations under [Saved prompt artifacts](runtime/prompt-only-core.md#saved-prompt-artifacts).
A layer's presence alone requires no retrieval entry.

## 4. Construct attention through relationships

### Large form, interval and degree

Choose the encounter the picture offers: being addressed, witnessing a private
moment, looking up at someone, entering an action, inspecting a design, or
discovering a world. Scale, crop, gaze, posture, shape and treatment make that
encounter concrete. A face need not lead every image. A person can remain its
subject while a hand, a name, an object or the surrounding world carries the
first impression.

Work with primary masses, their directions and the gaps between them. A dense
diagonal group needs space into which it can move. A small figure needs enough
contrast and surrounding interval to register as a presence in a large field.
A curved machine around a slender body creates different tension from several
small devices attached to that body. The latter can share the same capabilities
without producing the same picture.

Degree changes the construction. A face that almost fills the frame creates a
different proximity from a head with comfortable margins. A figure far smaller
than the word beneath it makes the word into a place. A foreground boot much
nearer than the face gives the viewer a physical position. Describe the actual
extent when it carries the effect: which mass is cut, what remains visible,
which interval is broad, which difference is extreme. Numerical ratios can
support a fixed layout; they do not replace these relationships.

Judge scale through the combined shape pattern. A standing figure and a seated
figure with the same height can have different weight because of their width,
pose, color and surrounding openings. A large letter with an open counter can
occupy much of a page while leaving it airy. Reducing the letter and adding
outer margins increases empty area but changes that encounter. Describe where
the principal masses approach, cross or withdraw from the frame and where
their internal spaces open. A size adjective alone leaves that organization
unresolved.

Think of negative space as a shaped interval with a role. It can open in front
of a gaze, receive the implied movement of a group, separate a face from dense
equipment, or give intricate machinery room to be inspected. Several kinds
can coexist. Empty area that starts after a horizontally truncated bust has a
different relation to the body from a field into which its gaze or silhouette
opens. A practical display area can remain deliberately quiet without becoming
an unrelated hole in the image.

When copy, subject, clothing or canvas changes, reconstruct the large pattern
before refining its contacts. A darker outfit can move the picture's weight;
a short word removes strokes and counters; a wider canvas changes the reach
of a diagonal. Preserve the selected movement, pressure, separation or openness
through the choices still available: block scale and direction, placement,
color allocation, crop or the shape of the intervals. Reproducing coordinates
alone does not preserve those effects. More clearance can be needed locally
without reducing every mass or regularizing the whole arrangement.

| Relationship | Constructive use | Consequence to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scale and contrast | A large quiet machine can surround a small bright indicator; a huge word can give scale to a small body | Physical or graphic scale, and the reason the smaller event remains noticeable |
| Value and silhouette | Join related dark planes into a coherent mass, then separate the contour where recognition needs it | Enough continuity to read the form without outlining every edge equally |
| Chroma and location | Let a restrained figure inhabit a saturated field, or concentrate color in a product and repeat it lightly elsewhere | Which region owns the color and what it connects; color need not be uniform illumination |
| Detail and rest | Put fine weave, joints or engraving inside broad forms and beside quieter skin, casing, cloth or background | Richness remains available without dissolving the main shape into equal texture |
| Edge and focus | Keep a contact or expression precise while neighboring edges soften, disappear or change mark | The necessary connection stays readable; blur and actual occlusion are different means |
| Direction and interruption | Hair, cloth, shadow, lettering and a contrasting short stroke can create movement around a still face | The different directions have a relationship, rather than each effect pointing independently |
| Repetition and difference | Repeat a curve, aperture, color role or material boundary while changing size, use and orientation | Belonging and individual function remain readable together |
| Omission and inference | Crop the eyes to concentrate on the lower face, or lose part of a body in darkness | The retained channel can carry the intended expression and still belongs to a coherent body |

These relationships influence attention; they do not guarantee a single scan
path for every viewer. Use viewing distance and intended size to decide which
differences need to survive the first glance and which can reward a longer
look.

Consider the scales between the largest forms and the smallest details.
A quieter middle-sized plane, repeated contour or broad tonal transition can
connect a dense subject to its surrounding field. Removing such a component
can leave the subject and background separately attractive but change the
whole rhythm. Reconstruct its contribution when that relationship is selected;
the same object, motif or number of accents need not recur. Leave it absent
when the selected construction works through a direct contrast of scales.

### Color and value as a shared construction

Read color through its distribution and behavior across the whole picture.
Hue names identify colors; their area, brightness, intensity, boundaries and
repetition establish what those colors do. A small yellow reflection, a large
flat yellow field and yellow replacing every lit plane can contain the same
hue while producing different pictures. Relate the broad light and dark masses
before refining their internal colors, and return to their combined effect as
the details develop.

Distinguish an object's local color from the color used to depict it. A brown
coat can be represented by black and cream ink; a face can share the blue of a
shadow field; hair, cloth and lettering can join one dark shape despite being
different things. A limited palette can reorganize all those contents. Keeping
their ordinary separate colors and adding a colored background does not retain
that organization. Apply design and representation at their actual scopes under
[Represent the selected design](runtime/render-contract.md#represent-the-selected-design).

Consider how the selected treatment assigns color, tone and edges:

| Construction | Relationship carried by the treatment |
| --- | --- |
| An achromatic subject in a colored field | The subject's values and marks remain distinct from the surrounding hue; colored light need not spill into it |
| A restricted set of inks across the whole image | Body, clothing, type and field share the chosen light, middle and dark colors; their boundaries can join or cut across depicted objects |
| Colored light transforming a scene | Source direction, surface response, reflected light and colored shadows connect the changes within depicted space |
| Selective chroma within a subdued image | A few surfaces or reflections carry intense color while the surrounding values support their concentration |
| Broad printed shapes with fine drawn detail | Coherent color and shadow masses carry the first impression; line, grain and small material variation remain inside or cross them deliberately |

These constructions can combine. Resolve their extent and boundaries instead
of applying every treatment uniformly. A sharply bounded color plane crossing
a face and the surrounding field behaves differently from a smooth light cast
on skin. An overexposed pale area can join hair, fabric and background by losing
their edges; a dark ink mass can join unrelated objects through a shared
silhouette. Restoring each object's full shading, color range or outline can
destroy that deliberate connection while making every object easier to inspect.

For example, a pale face, a deep violet jacket shadow and a violet word can
form a single diagonal against a flat peach field. Let the same violet enter
the hair shadow and a small facial plane, with cream reserved for the cheek,
a cloth fold and a letter counter. Thin neutral lines carry the hair and fabric
detail within those few large tones. Rendering natural skin gradients, a blue
jacket and brown hair separately would keep the subject inventory but change
the color logic. A commission for faithful garment colors would instead require
another treatment. The example establishes a relationship, not a preferred
palette.

### Proximity, gaze and the experience of a person

A close portrait can address the viewer or leave the viewer beside an absorbed
person. The same pleasant face does not create both relationships.

For a quiet, intimate graphic portrait, one possible construction places an
oversized profile at a side edge, crops the back of the head, and opens space
in the direction of the gaze. A hand-held object near the face gives that
interval a lived duration: heat rising from a cup, a fingertip paused over a
page, or a held token. The face, hand and interval belong together. Drawing the
person in graphite-like gray against a saturated color field assigns the color
to the surrounding atmosphere. An offset contour in a related color can join
portrait and field as print layers; a soft foreground form adds a different
distance. Precise eyes, fingers, fabric and hair can coexist with those large
simple masses. A strong heading may touch the hair without occupying the
face-hand interval. Changing the object or hue preserves this construction;
turning the eyes toward the viewer or separating the bust into its own rectangle
changes the intimacy it offers.

For an assertive fashion close-up, a face can turn away while the eyes meet the
viewer. Camera height, head direction and gaze are separate choices. A high
view with eyes lifted toward it differs from a low view with eyes lowered
toward it, even at the same head angle. Strongly colored translucent lenses
can form the main chromatic plane across a largely monochrome face and
clothing. Large shadow shapes and selective highlights give the lenses,
cheek, hair and collar a shared graphic force. Cropping hair, cheek or part of
the frame makes the face exceed the available space. Print on a shoulder can
continue the composition across its volume, as described under type below.
The selected degree of crop and monochrome treatment matters: a softly colored
bust with generous margins is another construction, even if it has glasses.

An editorial portrait can make a small change feel revealing. Removing a
familiar accessory and keeping it visibly held near the face preserves the
evidence of identity while showing a different moment. Bright natural light,
partly lost edges, translucent hair and a large soft foreground can concentrate
the remaining precision at the eyes or gesture. A formal title and a smaller
handwritten voice can give the same image public polish and personal intimacy.
More costume changes or more props can obscure the particular change that
made the portrait interesting. This is useful when a slight variation is the
commission; it is not a general limit on redesign.

When hands carry the image, start from an intelligible action and let view,
focus and contrast favor its contact. Fingers around a vessel, tension against
a strap, the pressure of a fist or a tool held just before use can reveal a
person without a dominant face. A nearby hand-object plane can remain sharp
while the face recedes in focus or value and looks elsewhere. The arm and
torso still explain where the hand belongs. Enlarging an isolated hand without
that connection supplies scale, not necessarily the action's emotional force.
Keep anatomy at the subject's declared body plan; a human example establishes
no digit count for a different subject.

Withholding the eyes can make a lower-face portrait unusually intimate or
unsettling. Crop close enough that the omission reads as the viewer's position,
then let mouth, jaw, neck, shoulder and any touching hand form one attitude.
Precise lips or a small corner movement can sit inside rougher painted fabric
and broad shadows. Small, widely spaced copy may slow the reading instead of
competing with the missing gaze. A masked or mouthless subject needs another
actual expressive channel; merely cutting off its eyes cannot supply a human
mouth's performance. Changing the crop to reveal a full face changes what the
viewer is asked to infer.

An expressive close-up can also depend on what stays unchanged. A deadpan face
rendered with the seriousness of a grand portrait can make a small, colloquial
wish funny. A masked face can carry resolve through head direction, the shape
of its eye region, the surrounding space and exact copy. The relationship
between image and words can supply emotional ambiguity, tension or irony
without adding a smile, tears or a stock expression.

### Shape and mark as one drawing grammar

Construct the visible forms at their different scales. Proportion, contour,
shadow shape, surface marks and the surrounding field make one grammar. A
medium name describes a family of possibilities; the chosen relationships
determine this picture. Detailed illustration can use highly simplified forms,
and a sparse image can preserve photographic volume. Detail density and the
degree of abstraction are independent choices.

Observe how a form is made, as well as which form is present. A head can have
large shaped eyes, a small nose mark and a short mouth line within a broad,
quiet cheek. Another treatment can model the same features with continuous
gradients, rounded lips and tiny surface variations. The former may contain
many precise lashes and hair strands without becoming the latter. Likewise,
a curved machine can be built from angular light planes, a few flat shell
colors and dense joint marks, or from smoothly reflective volumes. Preserve
selected proportions and structural identity at their actual scopes; a change
in rendering alone does not authorize a different body or age.

Relate those local choices to the large pattern. In a graphic ink treatment,
an angular coat shadow, an open white gap, a long hair silhouette and a large
colored word can share one directional rhythm. Broad black shapes can carry
as much form as outlines. A heavier outer contour, fine interior lines and
interrupted light-side edges perform different jobs. Halftone or paper grain
can bridge solid areas and fine lines without giving every region equal
texture. Their scale and placement determine whether the first impression is
the combined shape or a collection of surfaces.

Material names need a realization within this grammar. Knitted cloth might
appear as a few repeated dark strokes inside a pale plane, dense drawn cables
cut by a single shadow shape, or individually lit fibers. Metal might be
described by two hard reflection bands or a continuous reflected environment.
Specify the selected behavior where it matters instead of assuming that more
material detail preserves the reference more faithfully. Precise hair,
patterned cloth, polished shoes or mechanical joints can enrich broad masses;
quiet skin, casing or open paper can connect them. Equal hatching or shading
through every gap removes that relationship while retaining the objects.

Light also makes drawing shapes. A brilliant area can replace much of a cheek,
sleeve and adjacent field with the same pale plane, interrupting their contours
and internal texture. Another image keeps those forms intact and adds small
bright reflections to them. Both can depict strong sunlight, but only the
first makes that large opening in the picture. Describe the size, continuity
and edge behavior of the light and dark regions that organize the encounter.
The physical source direction does not by itself determine their drawn shape.
Where highlights fragment into sharp wedges, keep their connection to the
broader gesture; where light spreads softly, let the transitions carry that
continuity. Darkness can likewise join otherwise separate forms, as described
under [selective dissolution](#space-darkness-and-selective-dissolution).

In a soft luminous treatment, broad light areas, translucent passages and a
soft near plane can join forms while a few sharp features hold recognition.
In a painted treatment, a tightly resolved mouth or hand can sit among larger,
broken strokes; the change of scale lets a small action survive the paint's
energy. Roughness everywhere and precision everywhere each remove that
particular relationship. A photographic or fully modeled treatment can instead
make continuous material response the intended pleasure. Choose that behavior
when the commission calls for it, with its own distribution of focus and rest.

For equipment or objects, broad pale shell panels, narrow dark gaps,
transparent volumes, small metallic joins and quiet textile areas can organize
complexity together. A transparent core needs a boundary and interior; a
glossy shell needs reflections appropriate to its form; a matte surface can
remain quiet beside both. Translate these distinctions into the selected
marks and tones. Material separation and a unified graphic treatment can
coexist without turning every part into a separately polished object.

Carry this grammar in the depiction itself. For example, an enormous pale
wing, the white side of a face and an open paper interval can become one
ascending shape, crossed by a few dark feather strokes and a small eye mark.
Those details make the presence specific without closing the light shape.
Describing a fully shaded wing and a finely modeled face, then adding a broad
style adjective, leaves a different realization open. Describe the connected
shape and how its particular contents are drawn. Neither an inventory of
techniques nor instructions to finish every object independently establishes
that visible relationship.

### Stillness, weight and movement

Movement can belong to the figure, the surrounding forms or both. A still
fashion pose can be the calm center of a forceful poster: wind-driven hair and
cloth, angular shadows, rotated type and short crossing accents move around a
steady gaze. The character need not shout, run or fight for the picture to feel
energetic. Making the expression as violent as every surrounding shape can
remove the stabilizing contrast.

A low view of a seated figure can communicate possession of a place through
relaxed support. Near feet and knees lead toward a higher face; stairs, a seat
or architecture make the viewer's lower position intelligible. A heavy word
or band below can act as a graphic base. Perspective exaggeration can intensify
proximity when authorized, while visible support preserves ease rather than
instability. A throne changes the meaning of authority; the high-low relation
can also work on an ordinary step. Preserve whichever construction the request
or source actually selects.

For dance or another bodily action, let the ground and the body's changing
weight participate. A near shoe, a rear lifted foot, a turning torso and fabric
moving on another arc can make a low observer feel inside the motion. Wet ground
can reflect the large colors of a night setting, while blurred city lights and
clear type separate a dark costume without exposing every internal detail.
Neon behind an upright frontal figure does not create this bodily encounter.
The same construction can feel joyful, poised or tense through the actual
performance and treatment; nightlife supplies no automatic emotion.

A group can become one advancing form through depth. Put distinguishable
figures at consequentially different distances, with the nearest hand, foot
or tool projecting toward the viewer and intermediate bodies continuing the
direction. A dense cluster on one side can open toward a broad quiet field.
One concrete arrangement compresses the group down the right side: a small
distant head begins the chain high in the image, intermediate bodies overlap
below it, and the nearest hand and shoe project across the lower foreground.
Broad white space on the left receives the movement and a smaller title.
Repeated angular planes or controlled value masses unify the group, while
head shapes, local colors and equipment keep members distinct. Cropping the
nearest mass can extend the action beyond the frame. Equal-sized complete
figures in a row fulfill a different group-picture purpose and will not retain
the same thrust. A mirrored or differently directed arrangement needs its own
open interval; the example's side assignment is not a universal rule.

An action's smallest change can carry more tension than its largest motion.
For a weapon being drawn, a hand on the grip and another stabilizing the sheath
must explain the same unfinished action. A short exposed section and its
concentrated reflection can become the event, while the face and torso remain
controlled. More slash trails or a fully raised weapon would move the image
to a later, different moment. Resolve the real tool, joint and contact geometry
where it carries that distinction; fine ornament can enrich the still masses
around it.

### Space, darkness and selective dissolution

Quietness can be constructed at very different distances. A lower portrait
against a broad simplified sky lets a viewer dwell in atmosphere rather than
inspect a large face. Layered silhouettes, restrained color roles and a small
amount of copy can make the upper field active. A distant oversized motif can
remain almost a cloud or watermark, discovered after the person rather than
announcing itself as a logo. Bring that motif forward or fill the sky with
events and the image becomes more explanatory.

For a much more solitary construction, a small low figure can occupy a large
field with only a low horizon and broken reflections to establish a place.
For example, a crouching body near one side of the lower portion can sit below
a low luminous horizon, with a dark reflective floor beneath it and most of
the upper field left nearly unmodulated.
The figure's contrast, posture and ground connection have to make its smallness
read as scale, not disappearance. The broad field carries duration; the floor
carries weight and distance. Extra buildings, particles and titles may be
useful in another image but would consume the silence of this one.

Darkness can actively remove information. A high narrow light can reveal the
top of the head, shoulders and a few cloth planes while the lower body returns
to the surrounding black. A thin trajectory passing in front of and behind
the body can imply depth without a furnished background. Its terminal motif
and a graphic word can answer the figure's larger mass. Long or interrupted
letter strokes can continue the trajectory while gaps preserve the darkness.
Raising every shadow for inspection changes an apparition into an evenly readable
figure on a black backdrop. Required recognition must survive in the retained
contours; unnecessary visibility need not be restored.

A luminous encounter uses another organization. In a vertical field, a hand,
a profile and a symbol above and in front of the face can form a rising relation
that continues into smoke, liquid or light. The symbol is a second presence,
not a small decorative lamp. Its light reaches the appropriate face and hand
planes, while the unlit body and surrounding black remain part of the large
shape arrangement. A dominant luminous color and a distinct shadow family can
separate these roles. Flowing lettering can follow the surrounding curves at
a smaller intensity.

The degree of abstraction is a real choice. A legible magical object surrounded
by particles creates a depicted encounter. Letting its boundary merge with hair,
cloth and flowing color can make the encounter itself an abstract transformation.
Keep the relation between person and symbol readable while deciding what may
dissolve. A uniform glow around an otherwise intact ordinary portrait does not
supply that transformation. Protect required structural connections, including
the subject's actual hand or appendage construction, without forcing every
other edge to remain closed.

## 5. Give designed objects a visual logic

### Translate a property into a system

When equipment, furniture, clothing, companions or a world derives from supplied
material, identify what is being translated: a visible shape, an activity, a
capability, a material behavior, a personal meaning or a literal emblem. A
color or printed symbol can be sufficient when recognition is the purpose.
A more extensive design needs that source to affect how the new thing is
formed, used or experienced.

| Source contribution | Possible transformation | Evidence in the resulting design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A contour or recurring curve | Shell, frame, cushion, joint path, silhouette or letter rhythm | The shape organizes construction rather than appearing only as a decal |
| A capability or way of moving | Contact surface, articulation, sensing, balance, support or fictional effect | The body, mechanism and action agree about what has changed |
| An activity | Tool, grip, working space, storage, visible process or consequence | A viewer can connect the invented form to the supplied activity |
| A material behavior | Folding, stretching, translucency, erosion, buoyancy or rigidity | It changes both large form and local surface behavior |
| A personal theme or tension | Contrasting objects, an environmental rule, a scene, a symbolic accessory or related voices | The source remains recognizable through the new relationship |
| A collection of interests | An inhabited cluster, independent emblems, scenes or a hierarchy of information | The chosen organization determines how the interests become a person or a world |

These are ways to author a translation, not claims about hidden properties of
a real person or measured device performance. Keep supplied facts, adopted
fiction and authored interpretation distinct at their existing owners. An
invented class, ability, specification or psychological voice needs its actual
creative status; visual confidence is not factual evidence.

Develop source, new purpose, form, use and presentation together. A folding
shell can inform a case's hinges and overlap; printing its picture on a box is
a more literal relationship. An animal's sensing or contact behavior can
inform equipment without adding that animal's anatomy to the wearer. Direct
motifs remain available when they are wanted. There is no requirement that a
design be mechanical, metaphorical or unfamiliar.

### A wearer and a large mechanical extension

A powered costume can be a designed presence before it is an explanatory
diagram. The wearable silhouette, the body's attitude and the main mechanical
mass need to form one figure. The design can suggest a capability while making
the fantasy of wearing it visually compelling. An operating scene or a detail
diagram answers another question and should enter when that question belongs
to the commission.

Begin with the relationship between the wearer and the extension. A broad
curved assembly can wrap around a narrow body; a folded oval mechanism can
echo a springing pose; a fan of angular shells can give a quiet stance a
predatory reach. Decide how it is carried, connected and articulated, then
relate head equipment, costume cuts, fasteners, shoes and small markings to
its form language. One integrated device can contain many parts. Several
devices can also form an integrated design when the request calls for them;
device count comes from the chosen design, not a universal style rule.

Maintain a hierarchy of large, medium and small forms. A sweeping shell or
claw supplies the memorable outer shape. Garment panels and body openings let
the wearer remain legible within it. Joints, straps, seams and hardware reward
closer inspection. Broad smooth areas of skin, casing or cloth can provide
rest beside dense mechanisms. Repeating pale armor, dark joint gaps, a small
accent family and transparent or luminous material can join the parts without
giving every surface the same brightness.

An uncluttered field, a faint large emblem and a small family of labels can
present this combined silhouette as a complete designed object. Ground contact,
a thin shadow or a restrained reflection can establish weight without building
a scene around it. The background's restraint permits a rich costume and
mechanism. Replacing the main extension with practical small gadgets, and
replacing the field with a lived scene plus insets, can make a good use-oriented
design but changes the expressive construction.

A motif should change more than surface decoration when it governs the whole
fantasy. A springing creature can suggest compressed joints, a light poised
body and a mechanism ready to extend. A predatory creature can suggest a forward
weight shift, reach in the arms, angular head framing and heavy gripping parts.
A shambling undead motif can coordinate sagging limbs, patched cloth, hanging
components and a support structure that seems to carry reluctant weight. A
ghostly motif can coordinate lifted feet, relaxed hanging hands, translucent
cloth, buoyant arcs and visible air between parts. These are alternative bodily
experiences, not the same mechanism recolored. Preserve the subject's declared
anatomy and any limits on transformation while translating the motif.

For an orbital version, flotation and scale need their own construction. A
slender suited body inside a much larger curved shell, opaque panels beside
transparent volumes and a concentrated core can establish a body-mechanism
relationship against darkness. A distant planetary curve can explain why feet
have no ground. Material depth and internal luminous detail can supply opulence
while large panel shapes retain clarity. Small ears or a badge alone cannot
establish the extension's organizing form or the experience of weightlessness.

### Companions, collections and proliferation

To grow companions from a character's visual language, translate the shape,
material or behavior of a source detail into an individual being. A loop may
become a gripping limb, a clasp a jaw rhythm, a lens a large eye, or a folded
motif a wing. Decide how much of the source object remains recognizable.
Attaching a face to an unchanged object is one literal design; a new creature
using its structural vocabulary is another. Select the degree the commission
needs rather than treating either as automatically successful.

A crowded group becomes a scene through contact and reaction. Companions can
grip hair, pull a cheek, rest on a shoulder or lean against one another. Let
their different sizes and actions create a connected outer contour and a few
important gaps through which the main character reacts. Repeated eye shapes
or materials can identify a family while distinct colors and bodies keep its
members lively. Distributing them at equal intervals as floating icons changes
intrusion and intimacy into a collection display. How much of the central face
remains visible determines whether the feeling is playful company, engulfment
or something else.

An inhabited collection works differently. A compact seated character can be
surrounded by personally meaningful things at bodily distances: a near shoe,
a stack that supports an elbow, a sleeping companion, an overhead plant and
tools crossing the body in the foreground. Overlap and scale make a small place
to stay. An exposed hand at work may supply the activity while the face is
partly hidden in the collection; a different arrangement can open a quiet face
inside the same density. Compressing the body and enlarging nearby objects can
make abundance affectionate or comic. Quiet color and a focused activity can
make it restful. The number of interests does not decide this effect; their
spatial relationship does.

### Furniture, adornment and products

A personal seat translates shape language into support. The back, arms, shell,
cushion and base should receive the intended body. An oblique overhead view
can reveal the seat's enclosure and the body's relaxed asymmetry together.
Legs, a dropped arm, a hand at the face or an object on the lap make the design
into someone's place. Hard outer forms and soft contact surfaces can reconcile
a forceful visual identity with rest. A cushion painted in the character's
colors, with an upright figure standing beside it, preserves recognition but
loses that bodily ownership.

Character-derived jewelry or accessories need a different scale of translation.
A leaf, circuit, recurrent interval or personal activity can become a linked
joint, cut stone, clasp, chain rhythm or engraved edge. Decide which piece
carries the main meaning and how subordinate pieces belong to it. A close face
and nearby hand can make the object actively worn and presented, with a quiet
skin or casing area beside dense ornament. A pale luminous fashion treatment
can make the contact delicate; a severe crop, dark facets and controlled bright
metal can make it confrontational. Material, pose and viewing distance select
the kind of desire the accessory creates.

For a quiet jewelry advertisement, earrings and a bracelet can share one
natural gesture: fingers near the ear bring the wrist below the face in a
similar depth plane. The set can then be seen without enlarging the jewelry
or pushing a hand toward the lens. Matching links and small localized color
accents lead between ear, fingers and wrist. Soft backlight, restrained
foreground blur and a broad light field can give delicate metal room to be
examined, with a small brand mark in that space. Dense jewelry near the lens
would create another, more forceful kind of display.

Distinguish an object that flatters its wearer from a product whose construction
must also be seen. Glasses can lead the viewer through a face, while a side
view reveals a shaped temple and a close detail reveals its joint. Headphones
can appear small in an absorbed listening portrait and large as a separately
lit product. The repeated design must agree across those views. A transparent
lens, dark lens and opaque visor also create different access to the person;
their visual effect is more than a material label. Region relationships for
these advertisements are developed below.

### Vehicle scale, gravity and use

The vehicle, rider and setting can produce several distinct experiences. Choose
the one the commission or source actually calls for; a vehicle noun does not
select it.

| Intended encounter | Concrete construction | What would change the encounter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A machine with public presence | A close low three-quarter view makes the front mass and surface workmanship dominant; the rider or owner gives scale; a street and light ground the machine in a real place | A large frontal portrait with a small vehicle behind it moves the subject back to the person |
| A compact machine that feels playful | Shorten and thicken the body, enlarge defining wheels or shells, keep the rider comparatively small, and release the mass slightly above a readable shadow; a large word balances the machine | Normal proportions and full contact restore ordinary weight; violent smoke and banking shift the image toward speed |
| A valued companion at rest | A readable side or three-quarter body, stable ground contact, a person leaning, waiting or holding something ordinary, and broad quiet space around intricate material detail | A battle stance or a fully furnished busy backdrop consumes the time available to inspect the object |
| Acceleration entering the viewer's space | Rider posture, steering, bank, near mass, road direction, trailing cloth and smoke share a force; a tilted panel and selected boundary crossings can extend it onto the page | Speed lines behind a stationary side view do not establish the same force or proximity |

Stylization can change proportions while preserving a coherent operating
relationship. A rider still needs a plausible place, support and control for
the chosen action, unless an authored autonomous or fantastical mechanism
explains otherwise. Mechanical completeness and the artistic crop are separate:
a cropped large vehicle can feel continuous beyond the frame while being
insufficient as a full design inspection. Do not silently substitute one use
for the other.

### A personal world and what a hero faces

At environmental scale, a source motif can govern a phenomenon rather than
decorate a skyline. A recurring curve can become an enormous luminous current
across the sky; layered pages can become architecture; fragments of a statue
can reorganize a city into a new visual order. Let the transformed motif affect
scale, light, spatial rhythm and the small figure witnessing it. Ground planes,
foreground structures and reflections can make an impossible spectacle feel
like a place encountered. Scattering associated symbols through an ordinary
landscape does not create the same organizing world.

An expansive version places the witness and familiar objects low in the image,
keeps an ordinary city or shore near a low horizon, and gives the upper field
to one enormous transformed phenomenon. A road, rail or reflection connects
the nearby place to that phenomenon; broad quiet sky keeps its large curve
or mass readable. Detailed clouds and a wet inhabited foreground can remain
rich while subordinate to that relationship. Enlarging the witness into a
portrait would turn the same spectacle into a backdrop.

A hero seen from behind can give the viewer a position beside that figure,
facing what matters. A small still body and a distant field of many lives or
activities establish the difference in scale. The environment needs concrete
meaning: workshops and inhabited rooms, cultivated land, a damaged city, a
planet, or another actual object of care. A brief word can bind the vista to
purpose without explaining every detail. Turning the figure back toward the
viewer changes the relation to an addressed portrait; replacing the specific
world with generic beauty can preserve spectacle while losing the reason for
this hero to stand there.

For a newly invented hero, translate activities into operations. What the
person actually does can become a fictional tool, an action performed with it,
and a visible effect on others or the environment. Writing can become drawing
a usable route; maintaining things can become restoring connections; gathering
knowledge can become making distant information available. The costume, main
and supporting equipment, stance and scene need to agree about that operation.
An occupational word on the chest supplies recognition but not this ability.
Large names or class graphics can present the design, while the class itself
retains only the authority supplied by the task's fictional rules or facts.

## 6. Make type and images work together

### Language, shape and voice

Separate literal copy from what lettering does in the picture. Preserve exact
requested words, punctuation and meaningful line breaks. A name, title, spoken
thought, label and incidental inscription have different jobs. Decide what
must be read immediately and what can be discovered through a longer look.

| Role of type | Construction to resolve |
| --- | --- |
| Message or title on the image surface | Reading order, size at the intended viewing distance, contrast, spacing and competition with detail |
| Dominant graphic form | Word and line silhouettes, repeated intervals, counterforms and the relation of the image to those shapes |
| Element interacting with a subject | Contact, support, overlap and foreground/background order, with a deliberate graphic or depicted relation |
| Print on a curved or folded surface | The carrying volume, major folds, perspective, text continuity and remaining legibility |
| Object or mark in an environment | Scale, material, attachment, illumination and occlusion within that environment |
| Information accompanying a product or diagram | Correspondence between labels and views, actual content and hierarchy |
| A voice that changes the image's reading | The relation between the words' register and the depicted expression, scale and atmosphere |

Large type is not one visual effect. A condensed word can stand beside a body
as a second vertical mass; a rotated word can press across the normal reading
direction; filled and outlined letters can vary density while retaining one
word; a restrained serif title can add formality beside a personal handwritten
line. Choose the construction and actual letter behavior, not an interchangeable
stack of font adjectives. Different scripts and word lengths change the shapes
available for it.

Equal outer dimensions do not give solid and outline lettering equal visual
weight. A filled area can anchor a composition while the open interior of an
outlined portion exposes the field or a crossing contour. Fine rules can align
those large masses with smaller information or interrupt their density. Their
function is structural when the selected page depends on those relationships.
For changed copy, reconstruct the needed density and alignment through the
available glyphs, spacing, fills, outlines and nonverbal shapes. Omitting a
source subtitle need not collapse the whole hierarchy into one uniform mass,
and preserving that hierarchy does not authorize inventing replacement copy.

Rotating a word as a whole, for example through a quarter turn, preserves its
internal order while making a strong vertical block. Filled strokes can conceal
the figure where they cross, while selected outline or translucent portions
produce another density. Choose their interaction under
[visibility and surface order](#visibility-and-surface-order). The original
figure's complete contour is not a prerequisite for composing the word.

The words can support, redirect or complicate an expression. A terse promise
can make a masked face read as protective rather than threatening. An ordinary
comic desire can interrupt the grandeur of a solemn portrait. Replacing either
with a generally uplifting slogan can keep the image attractive while losing
its particular voice. The type's size, placement and manner of speaking must
belong to that relationship.

### Letters that organize or become a place

When letters build the picture, consider the word's overall mass and its inner
gaps before fitting the subject around it. A large dark or colored word can
oppose the figure's silhouette; a crossing short line or stroke can interrupt
its long rhythm. Partial overlap can place type and clothing in the same
graphic field. Strong black-white shapes may continue across the boundaries
of figure and type, so the viewer alternates between reading language and
seeing the combined pattern. Independent text boxes would separate that
experience even when the copy and colors stay the same.

For a body inhabiting a name, enlarge the word until its strokes and counters
have the scale of a place. Select a contour that can receive the pelvis, hand
or foot, and arrange the body around that relation. Hanging legs, a palm supporting the body, fabric crossing the letter and a gaze opening into the remaining
field can make a small figure feel fully present. The letters can stay flat
and opaque; the contacts imply support at the boundary between graphic and
depicted space. Extruded stone type would create a different, fully physical
stage. A figure pasted over a large word without intelligible support supplies
overlap alone.

Use the actual copy to resolve line breaks and available supports. A new
language or a longer phrase can require another arrangement of word blocks,
gaps and body placement. Resolve the mass and direction of each block as well
as the local seat. One band can descend across a page while another rises,
or several unequal blocks can pass a common direction across distinct regions.
Their starts, ends, angles, color weight and intervals create that movement.
A steady seated body can oppose it. Locally correct support and a few drifting
motifs do not supply movement missing from the principal blocks.

Keep glyphs and typesetting coherent within each block while choosing the
blocks' relationship. A straight baseline can be inclined relative to the page;
stable spacing does not require every line to share a horizontal direction.
Select a usable part of the resulting contour for the body's support. If it
does not work, adjust an open block, line break, sitting location or body
orientation together. A convenient horizontal seat should not silently decide
the entire page's direction. When the commission instead calls for a stable
rectilinear arrangement, that relationship remains the one to construct.

Use counterforms and the intervals between blocks as part of the open field.
The letters can approach or partly cross the frame, with openness carried by
their interiors or by a selected gap elsewhere. A body can occupy one of those
gaps, cross a filled stroke or disappear partly beneath it. Broad exterior
margins create another relationship and belong when that is the selected effect.
Choose what the viewer needs to recover through the crossings, including the
needed reading order and distinctive strokes.

Color repeated between costume and type can make the huge word feel like part
of the person's world. Shared boundaries, connected color masses and deliberate
interruptions can join them, while another boundary supplies a necessary face
fragment, gesture or reading cue. The subject can retain finely drawn volume
within a construction organized by flat type.
Smaller motifs, quiet intermediate shapes or thin arcs can connect distant
regions without taking over the large flow. Choose their role and strength
from the whole construction, not from a required count of decorations.

### Lettering carried by a body or a place

For surface print, design the graphic and its carrying volume together. A line
can cross a shoulder into a chest or follow neighboring garment panels; the
chosen pose and crop determine its reach across the picture. Carry baseline,
spacing and reading direction through the large volume changes, with compression
on the far side and interruption by folds or straps. Resolve the selected
balance of graphic force, legibility and bodily volume. A perfectly frontal
word can detach from the garment, while uniform distortion at every small
wrinkle can dissolve its shape. The print can be the main pattern that the body
animates. Choose its line breaks, scale, planes and treatment through that
relationship while retaining fixed design requirements.

A wall graphic involves another scale. A logo and lettering much larger than
their maker can be experienced as completed work in a real place. The body
resting beside it, a low viewing position, floor-to-wall depth and selected
traces of making establish authorship and achievement. Shape and color can
move from the creator's appearance into the graphic while paint remains part
of the wall material. Daylight and leaf shadows, or a night scene with colored
light, are alternative treatments of this relation. Reproducing the letters
accurately while losing the maker, scale and site changes it into a wall used
as a decorative portrait background.

Keep exact text as an independent requirement during method selection. Attractive
lettering does not prove accurate spelling, small labels or repeated copy.
Use an evidenced image input, layout control, separate typesetting or editing
operation when the actual tools support it. Record and inspect the real output.
Do not silently shorten fixed copy, omit it, or claim a proposed later operation
has happened.

## 7. Organize several views or information units

### The relation between regions

Give regions different responsibilities when the work needs different readings:
recognition, activity, context, emotion, mechanism, comparison or atmosphere.
Their size, treatment and boundaries can make a person or design richer than
any one view. Repeating the same subject in several rectangles is not by itself
an editorial construction.

Establish shared identity or design once, then choose what each view reveals.
A detail must correspond to the part in the whole. Reading order does not
necessarily advance time. Maintain real sequence continuity when the request
does require it. Decide whether regions meet through borders, gaps, overlap,
a common surface or continuous space, and carry that relation into the prompt.

### An editorial portrait and a story montage

An editorial portrait can place one large recognizable figure across unequal
windows into that person's life. A larger setting establishes a world; smaller
work and leisure scenes show different modes of living; a close expression
returns attention to the person; objects and a simplified emblem condense
identity at another level. Let the large figure cross selected boundaries so
the page belongs to one person rather than to unrelated pictures. A name can
be a major shape behind that figure. Repeated color roles, contour treatment
and motifs unite the page while a monochrome face or a flat icon supplies
a deliberate change in how it is read.

The windows need not all have the same style, size or amount of information.
A detailed color scene, a quiet expression and a simple symbol can make a
rhythm of encounter, recognition and pause. If every region becomes a softly
painted daily activity, the page may explain the biography but lose the
contrast that makes it an edited portrait. If the central figure dominates
every window, the advertised breadth of the person can disappear. Exact panel
counts and proportions follow the chosen source or commission.

A story montage has another job: fragments invite inference about a larger
event or a double life. An action, a tool in use, a masked close-up, an ordinary
social moment, an adversary and a revealing detail can imply relationships
without spelling out a chronological strip. Recurring equipment and visual
motifs join those fragments; unequal scale and directional cuts establish a
publicity image's emphasis. All-action fragments can remove the contrast
between private life and extraordinary role. Even a persuasive montage cannot
visually prove an off-frame fact such as who knows a secret; preserve that
difference between depicted evidence and story premise.

### A collection of declarations and a map of voices

Sticker-like images retain independence inside a shared personality. Distinct
cut edges, surrounding gaps, varied rotation and selective overlap let scenes,
objects and phrases be handled as separate small statements. A central avatar
can organize them, but their individual meanings must remain different.
Use scale and recurrence to connect the collection without flattening it into
equal labels. Removing all gaps and cut edges can turn it into an inhabited
pile of things; boxing everything in the same grid changes the collected,
portable quality.

A map of personal concerns can use words as unequal voices. A large central
wish, smaller competing wishes, hesitations, arrows, handwritten additions and
small ordinary scenes can make contradiction readable. Large assertions and
later qualifications create a different time of reading from a uniform table.
Specific supplied facts can ground the composition, while invented inner voices
remain playful interpretation, not a verified diagnosis. Reusing a flattering
central phrase for every person discards the source's particular tension.
Removing every contradiction creates a different, purely celebratory work.

### Experience and inspection in a product image

A worn product and an isolated view can reinforce each other. An absorbed
listening portrait makes headphones desirable as an experience; a large object
view shows the cup, band and material that the portrait cannot expose. Shared
color, a continuing surface or a product crossing the boundary can join them
into one advertisement. A quiet diagram region beside an expressive glasses
portrait can instead suggest careful design, with front, side and joint views
that all describe the same object. Small labels support correspondence; their
mere presence does not establish mechanical clarity or real performance.

Allocate space for the encounter the image should sustain. A large explanatory
inset can draw attention away from the person and make a scene into a product
study. That can be right for the commission. It is not an automatic improvement
to an image whose primary pleasure was the body's integrated silhouette or
the atmosphere of use. Keep the emotional use and precise inspection distinct
enough to serve their roles, and connected enough to make the same object
desirable and understandable.

### Several deliverables with different jobs

An action image and a reusable design sheet can be intentionally separate.
The action image establishes the identity through stance, tool use, scale and
the effects of a capability. The sheet exposes front, back and equipment in
views suitable for understanding the same design. Dramatic concealment that
serves the first image can obstruct the second; importing all of the sheet's
views into the first can dissolve its force.

Use the selected design and actual reference authority across those outputs.
Resolve previously unseen surfaces as additions to the same design, not a new
conception prompted with the same name. The existing production workflow owns
their dependencies, actual images and review. Describing two images in one
prompt does not establish that two separate deliverables were generated.

## 8. Resolve competition and choose a realizable method

Identify what each consequential choice protects. User requirements and adopted
definitions retain their authority; an agent-selected arrangement is adjustable.
When parts compete, change the relationship that is open: consolidate a large
mass, redistribute detail, move secondary copy, vary a boundary, change the
view, or choose another coherent construction. Both simplification and increased
richness can be valid repairs. Preserve the content and effect that justified
the choice rather than defending a technique label.

Repair at the scale of the missing relationship. If a body never occupies its
lettering, sharper text will not create the support. If a large mechanical
extension has become several small gadgets, extra screws will not restore the
combined silhouette. If a world has become a row of emblems, atmospheric glow
will not make it inhabitable. Local anatomy, spelling and material corrections
remain necessary when those are the actual problems; their success does not
establish a different whole-picture claim.

Carry the construction through [interface rendition](runtime/prompt-writing-guide.md#interface-rendition).
A descriptive model can receive explicit relationships in prose. A tag rendition
uses its own evidenced vocabulary and syntax to approach the whole construction
at the precision the target supports. Select concepts for its organization and
treatment as well as its objects. Carry relationships through supported concept
combinations, necessary phrases, references or controls, including prose in a
compatible mixed rendition; retain uncontrolled relationships as limits.
Input mode alone guarantees neither exact binding nor layout, counts or readable
text. Reference, regional or composition operations have separate contracts and
must be available before they are offered as means. An unexecuted operation
remains a proposal, and does not replace a requested tag rendition.

Inspect the complete final input with its actual reference roles. The organizing
form, scale, contact, contrast and regional relationships are the basis for
assessing what the rendition preserves and where its means are insufficient.
Express them through the selected form without including the author's
investigation or explanations. A study reduced to a subject name and a finish
label has not carried its construction into the input; a detailed sentence has
not established control that the target lacks.

## 9. Observation contract

Use [Production review](runtime/production-review.md) for the complete artifact
and its independent source basis. Read the actual image before judging the
intended effect. A technically valid request and an author's self-assessment
do not establish what the generated picture does.

The construction knowledge here helps interpret observed differences. Compare
the viewer's position, the large forms and intervals, the kinds of movement
or stillness, and the interplay of treatments before attributing the result to
one attractive detail. Then inspect necessary local connections. A small text
error and a change from a graphic portrait to a narrative scene have different
scope and need different repairs.

When finishing layers contribute through an image or SVG reference, record their actual behavior
in the relevant vocabulary, their role in the requested transfer, and any
conflict with the target treatment. Wet highlights belong to material response;
a vignette belongs to presentation; a graphic offset silhouette need not imply
a physically cast shadow. Preserve, rescope or replace a contribution according
to the selected source authority instead of averaging incompatible treatments.
A source's finish does not become the subject's identity.

Source instructions and source images can support different conclusions. A
request for bold simplified cel shading may accompany an image with fine hair,
patterned cloth and smooth volume inside strong large shapes. Reading only the
style label can lose the observed craft; copying the image cannot prove every
instruction was fulfilled. Keep those meanings separate under the actual
commission, including when a completed example contains a successful departure.

Judge the image at its intended size and in relevant detail. Retain an
unsuccessful first result and the actual repair under the existing production
review. A borrowed construction remains evidence within its observed scope;
its adaptation to another subject or target requires observation of that result.

## 10. Layer-specific cautions

- Type and logo elements have letterform, weight, material and effect vocabulary of their own. Distinguish requested text from unrequested watermarks and stray signatures. For freely authored copy, fewer or larger words can be a useful choice on a weak-text target. Fixed copy remains fixed; resolve its actual delivery through method selection.
- Environment-forward frames need the same construction care as a character: relevant depth planes, scale, sky, weather, atmosphere and the particular rule that makes this place the intended world. A location noun alone does not supply them.
- Renderer names, such as octane or another engine, are usually style cues on image models rather than literal renderer selection. Describe the visible qualities that matter, such as broad reflections, ambient occlusion, surface edges or display lighting, alongside or instead of the name.
- Quality vocabulary belongs to the target interface's convention. A quality-tag stack supplies no missing silhouette, action, light arrangement, material relation or page construction.
