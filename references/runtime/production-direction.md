# Production direction

Establish the requested work and the result it must provide: an artifact,
an assessment, or a change to an artifact. Understand what the audience should
notice or experience, the fixed requirements and the agent's creative scope.
Read the applicable design, persona and authorial intent together. A trait,
emotion or format label alone does not determine its expression. Character
creation and portrayal start from a concrete definition of the character;
supporting work keeps its commissioned scope. A story clock and a physical
camera are optional.

Develop intended content and visual construction together: what exists, what
happens, how its parts relate, and how the picture presents them. Use adopted properties as the subject's
definition and make open creative choices within delegated authority. Carry
the chosen realization into the artifact. A recommendation identifies the
selected course and the substantive reason it serves the commission; a prompt
describes the requested depiction. Include a qualification when it changes
the content, choice, action or use of the deliverable. Keep investigation
history and routine confidence commentary in the authoring context.

Develop, write and review the depiction through [Authoring work](authoring-work.md),
including a prompt delivered in conversation. Compose the actual candidate from
the current request, applicable sources and chosen construction. Amend that
candidate when the request retains particular choices; compose a new candidate
when the work calls for a different construction. The same author judges and
performs both operations. Studio saves or executes that work when requested.

## Establish the character for this use

Character Prompt Builder develops characters and their portrayals. When an
open request supplies only a mood, place or loose idea, use it to develop a
character proposal and the circumstances that make that character worth
depicting. The agent takes on the open design choices within the request's
creative scope. A setting alone, a headcount, a name or an unnamed figure
added for scale does not establish the character the work is about.

Read the complete applicable character definition and its source roles when
one exists. Preserve what is settled and develop the parts this use leaves
open. When there is no definition, build a coherent working one as part of
conceiving the picture. Resolve the distinctions needed for the current
proposal or depiction, and keep other choices open. Kind and embodiment,
recognizable appearance, disposition or purpose, and the concrete state or
behavior are possible areas to develop, not an intake form to complete before
showing an idea. A design study can concentrate on form; an interaction needs
the character's relation to that situation. A role or emotion label leaves
its individual realization to be chosen when that realization is used.

Let the definition grow with the work. The first useful proposal need not
settle a name, biography, complete appearance, every expressive form or a
future scene. Make coherent provisional choices within the user's delegation
so the user can react to an actual idea. Develop or revise the relevant
definition when a later scene, visible detail or requested variation needs
it. Keep proposed details distinct from supplied or adopted facts; a choice
made for one image does not close the character's remaining possibilities.

A character can be human, animal, machine, creature, a personified object or
another designed entity. Establish the actual body or assembly and the
channels it has for acting or expressing itself. A face, human anatomy and
human emotional behavior are not prerequisites. For a depicted expression,
use [Character Performance](character-performance.md) to select a concrete
combination and degree from this character's defined forms, or to develop
those forms where the design is open. An emotional category alone does not
define that combination.

While making these choices, follow
[Define a character with usable wording](prompt-vocabulary.md#define-a-character-with-usable-wording).
Investigate relevant dictionary meanings and keep suitable keywords and any
necessary authored phrases beside the concrete definitions they express.
When showing a concrete proposal, include those associations briefly with
the relevant details, distinguishing inspected terms from authored phrases.
They make the definition usable later; they do not ask the user to choose tags.
The agent does this work; the user can speak in ordinary language or delegate
the design. Ask a short question when its answer would change an important
choice that the available information and authority cannot settle. Group
related uncertainty into that useful choice instead of asking about every
property in turn. Otherwise make a usable proposal and proceed. Do not make
uncertainty about later details a reason to delay the first idea or assign a
Persona questionnaire or vocabulary research to the user.

For one picture or a provisional design, a short inline definition is enough
when it supplies the distinctions that depiction needs. Keep it in the
conversation or existing authoring record and carry its relevant content into
the actual prompt or delivered references. A name, saved Studio, character ID,
complete Persona dossier and canonical adoption are separate choices; none
is a prerequisite for that proposal. Existing definitions retain their owners.
When a continuing project needs fuller portrayal or registration, use
[Cast Admission and Persona Depth](cast-and-persona-depth.md). Developing a
working proposal does not silently adopt its details as permanent canon.

Compose from this character's presence, form, activity and situation. The
setting, other participants and visual treatment can be substantial parts of
the result while contributing to that portrayal. Character focus is a
relationship within the work, not a requirement for a large, central,
front-facing or fully visible figure. Compare the proposed picture with the
brief: what does its construction let the viewer encounter about this
character? Token inclusion of a figure supplies no answer.

An explicit request for a setting, background, prop, reference preparation or
other supporting artifact keeps that scope and can contain no depicted
character. World development can also precede a character design. Use any
known character requirements that matter to the support work, without
inventing a cast merely to finish it. A bounded edit or assessment preserves
its actual subject and authorized scope. These tasks do not settle an open
character commission as a landscape; return to the character definition when
the commissioned work proceeds to its portrayal.

## Translate established facts into the current appearance

Keep four owners distinct while turning the character into a depiction:

| Owner | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Canonical definition | Established age, history, disposition, relationships and adopted body design, with their actual authority |
| Current appearance goal | The forms, proportions, expression, action, clothing and visible relationships this image should show |
| Model input | Supported terms, phrases, weights and reference instructions chosen to produce that appearance |
| Returned-image evidence | The shapes, colors, textures, relationships and construction actually visible in the result |

A prompt controls the depicted appearance. Its vocabulary does not establish
or revise the character's underlying facts. Translate background into the
visual consequences needed for this image: posture, gaze, contact, object
placement, clothing fit, color or motion. A diagnosis, profession or biography
alone does not specify those consequences. Visible relationships include the
whole image, not only the face or costume. A visible quality can be assessed
without being reducible to a numerical measurement.

Choose physique directly when physique is the requested change. Relevant
relationships can include shoulder width relative to the head, torso depth,
limb thickness, muscle definition, head-to-body ratio and garment ease. An age
label does not determine these independently. Carry an occluded property into
the input when it affects silhouette, contact, draping or another required
visible consequence; enumerating hidden details is not itself coverage.

For example, an established adult character may have a deliberately slight
build and youthful facial proportions. A synthetic appearance instruction is:
"Keep the rounded cheeks, short jaw and narrow shoulders. Reduce the chest
bulk relative to the reference while retaining the head-to-body ratio and
the established facial markings." This changes the selected appearance within
its authorized scope; it makes no new assertion about age, health or biography.
An adopted body design still constrains which changes are available. Apply a
requested canonical change through its owning definition and adoption decision.

The actual depiction remains subject to the host's content rules. A canonical
adult age does not independently authorize a sexualized youthful or childlike
appearance; assess the requested appearance and context, not the biography
label alone. Preserve permitted appearance choices without using them to
bypass a content restriction.

Use [Interface rendition](prompt-writing-guide.md#interface-rendition) for
model-facing terms and [Production review](production-review.md#apply-the-prepared-review-basis)
for the resulting image. Existing direction, prompt, reference scope and review
records carry these distinctions. Add a sense note only where an ambiguity
affects the result; this introduces no per-word checklist or new approval file.

## Choose the expression

State the intended emotion directly when it is part of the picture. "The
character looks happy, with a slight smile" is a valid instruction; it need
not specify every muscle or replace happiness with a metaphor. Add the
character's distinctive visible response when the commission or adopted source
requires it. A source example supplies one possible response under its stated
conditions, not a universal ear angle, tail position or reaction sequence.

Compose the subject, action, expression, relationships and framing as one
picture. For a still, select a simultaneous visible state. Keep unshown timing,
thoughts, intentions, history and causes in their author-facing records, or
include only the scene premise needed to understand the depiction. A necessary
premise does not become a fact that the image proves. Supply a comparison
baseline for "as usual" or "more than normal"; otherwise resolve what appearance
is actually wanted before requiring that comparison.

A figurative impression may enrich an independently understandable depiction.
"The character looks happy, with a slight smile, as if a pleasant thought has
just crossed their mind" has a direct primary expression. The suggested thought
and timing remain secondary impressions, not facts to certify from the image.
Remove the figurative clause mentally and check that the main picture remains
clear. No particular phrase is required or checked by keyword.

Add exclusions when the request, an applicable source, reference role or an
observed failure supplies their purpose. Do not invent an opposing emotion or
pose solely to explain the positive one. When correcting a premise, reassess
the dependent direction, prompt and criteria together. Preserve the intended
reaction and individuality; neither fewer words nor more geometric details is
the purpose of that correction.

Work on the depiction the user is commissioning. This document owns that
creative work from interpretation through revision. Composition resources,
presets, performance knowledge and interface guides contribute to it when a
question needs their knowledge; they do not each start another design process.
Use the actual candidate as the working surface. Its selected expression and
realization carry the construction; notes explain a choice when that explanation
helps the current work. Resolve details as they become necessary for the artifact.

When the commission supplies only a topic, use
[Form a prompt from an idea](#form-a-prompt-from-an-idea) to develop its open
choices. Retain the requested content, subject roles and kind of work. The
agent chooses an intended effect within that commission; an unstated use
remains unselected.

Read the request and supplied material in their actual roles. A brief says what
the work must provide. An adopted design supplies the properties covered by
its adoption, including those carried into a later request without restatement.
Apply the current state, authorized changes and necessary conditions of the
intended use at their own scopes. A draft proposes a realization. Commands inside that draft are proposed
instructions to a renderer, not commands to its reviewer to preserve them.
An image may supply design,
performance, technique or another assigned contribution, and may be available
only to the author. Read complete sources where their meaning depends on the
whole. Establish what is fixed, what can be chosen and what operation is
requested before changing a candidate. Assessment uses
[Observe and repair](#observe-and-repair); it need not produce a rewrite.

Develop the picture in terms of what is there and what it means in context.
For a scene, understand the situation being lived by its participants: what
they are doing, what they attend or respond to, and how their circumstances
make the action intelligible. Use ordinary practical and social understanding,
including what the participants can perceive, support, reach or affect in that
situation. For a design, explanation or graphic artifact, understand the form,
function, comparison or experience its parts create within the established
scope. A story clock and a physical camera are optional. An emotion,
occupation or format label can
supply useful meaning, but does not choose every aspect of its realization.

Keep that understanding intact while making the depiction concrete. An action
such as shielding a flame from a draft already relates a person, hands, flame
and air. Use the action as an intelligible unit. Develop a particular grip,
orientation or clearance when it changes how the action works or is read, or
when the request fixes it. Deriving every joint position from an action and
turning those consequences into separate requirements replaces the useful
meaning with an unnecessarily restricted pose. The same applies to treating a
generic mood as a universal facial recipe or a design as unrelated attributes.
For an individualized expression, use [Character Performance](character-performance.md)
to define its concrete form and permitted range, then select the form under
these circumstances. Carry that chosen combination into the actual text or
supplied media. `Happy`, `smiling` or `embarrassed` alone leaves the individual's
expression open.
Technical detail is useful when it resolves a real construction or expression
choice; technical-sounding precision alone contributes no choice.

Choose the visible realization by inhabiting that situation or understanding
that design. The activity, reaction, arrangement and treatment must belong to
the same picture. A participant's posture follows what they are doing with
something; an object's placement follows its role; the view presents those
relationships. Work between content and presentation as needed. A camera or
layout chosen early remains open when its consequences work against the
commission. Do not alter the intended activity to protect a convenient view.
A specified view or exact detail can itself be the commission and then governs
the construction. Follow consequential dependencies together using the relevant
[composition knowledge](prompt-composition.md#construction-order).

Select specificity through the decision being made. Explicit fine requirements
retain their full precision. Chosen detail can establish identity, distinguish
an action, enrich an atmosphere, guide attention or supply the substance of a
complex design. These functions can justify extensive description. Ordinary
consequences of an already intelligible construction can remain with the
renderer. A source can require faithful construction without requiring all
of its surfaces to be exposed. A freely chosen number, angle or landmark does
not become necessary because it is measurable. Do not require a separate
justification record for every word or detail; author the picture at the
resolution its requirements and expression call for.

Use the actual source of authority while making these choices. Preserve user
requirements and adopted properties, including small features, exclusions and
requested ambiguity. An agent's choice of pose, crop, emphasis or convention
is a means within that authority. Its appearance in an earlier draft, record
or review does not promote it into a requirement. A local edit preserves the
rest of the baseline; a request to reconsider the depiction can reopen the
means that organize it. Establish that scope before projecting it into a
revision record. Keep identity and story adoption at their existing owners.

Consult knowledge for a question encountered in this work. A supplied design
or an already understood situation can be sufficient. Use
[Discovery](sparse-discovery.md) when an expressive choice benefits from
possibilities; use focused catalog, source or vocabulary inspection for a
specific construction or wording need. Read complete selected records and
adapt their meaning to this depiction. Incidental subjects, palettes, cameras
and example arrangements do not transfer with the useful knowledge. A search
result, a filled specification field and an available specialist are not new
obligations to add content. Conclude an investigation when its question is
resolved and continue constructing the picture.

Choose presentation for the actual use and viewing conditions. A page, card,
screen or display determines what can be encountered and read together.
Distinguish an artifact from an image of it in a setting. A visible interface
is not evidence of working interaction. Arrangement, attention, sequence,
typography and relationships remain design choices alongside rendering medium.
For open choices, compare alternatives when their effects offer a useful
tradeoff. A fixed layout or sufficient routine solution needs no manufactured
contest. Select the alternative that best serves the requested result and
carry its realization into the work. Explain the determining advantage when
giving a recommendation. Other suitable alternatives can remain available
while the selected one proceeds. Resolve a question
before that choice when its answer could change the selection or a fixed
requirement; otherwise complete the chosen realization. Choose within delegated
scope and proceed; a consequential choice alone does not require approval.
Ask when unresolved meaning or authority would change a fixed requirement,
or when the user requested selection.

Resolve hierarchy and local construction together. What is noticed first,
what sustains attention and what supplies supporting information depend on the
commission. Subject importance does not assign surface order or an unobstructed
area. A word, object, shape or image fragment can pass in front of the main
subject, across it or behind it; choose those relations with scale, crop, color
and treatment as one construction. Rich ornament, quiet space, precise workmanship
or expressive exaggeration may carry the effect. There is no universal preference
for simple images, prominent faces, wide framing or a low detail count. Use
[representation grammar](prompt-composition.md#representation-grammar) and
[render intent](render-contract.md#choose-the-visual-treatment) for the selected marks,
materials and treatment. A medium label alone does not settle the composition.

For multiple regions or treatments, give each a role in the whole. Decide how
they meet: shared depicted space, a common page surface, overlap, a detail
view, a transition or another authored relation. Reading order need not advance
time. Shared identity can recur with different pose, crop and visible surfaces.
A photographic setting containing a drawn figure needs its chosen contact,
scale and depth; a collage can deliberately place photographs and notes on a
shared graphic surface. Neither requires panel borders. Flat diagrams and
type need visual organization without invented anatomy or physical cameras.
Use the existing Production Specification for any required local detail.

### Carry the chosen depiction into the input

Write for the renderer's actual task and information. Preserve the situation,
relationships, qualities and degree in meaningful language as they pass into
the prompt. State the premises the depiction needs, assign delivered images
their selected roles, and translate necessary contributions from authoring-only
sources into text. Do not mechanically translate each planning field into a
sentence. Knowledge that helps the author decide can remain outside the input
when it supplies no necessary content to the commissioned image. Production
history, approvals and reasons for choosing one solution remain in their
records. Relevant prior events or off-frame circumstances can belong in a
prompt when they make the requested depiction intelligible; temporal position
alone does not determine relevance.

Apply [interface rendition](prompt-writing-guide.md) to that authored meaning.
The master prompt can be the final portable text; it does not require a second
artifact or a preliminary attribute list. Exact parser grammar may change
syntax, never the situation or meaning it is meant to communicate.

### Choose framing from the intended effect

Determine whether the prompt needs to select a view. Requested or adopted
framing remains fixed. Author a view when its spatial relationships or
expressive effect are part of the proposed depiction; otherwise the model may
compose the specified content. A complete first proposal need not name
distance, crop, placement, scale or margins.

For a view the prompt establishes, decide what the viewer should experience
or understand and choose framing that makes that experience work. Resolve its
spatial choices together with the depicted activity or design. When selecting
an open view, consider a materially different realization if it would change
the effect, then select one for its contribution to this commission. Carry
that chosen view into the first proposal; the user need not reject a stock
frame before receiving a directed picture. Explain the determining reason
briefly when presenting the direction. A fixed or already sufficient view
needs no invented alternatives or additional approval.

Give the frame the visible forms and relationships that carry this encounter,
gesture, material detail, design inspection or relationship to a place. Describe
their extent, contact, overlap and movement through the image. When a form
continues across an edge, its visible continuation establishes the crop. The
whole character remains defined in the sources; the frame selects an encounter
with that character. An inspection may need a complete figure, while another
use may need the space around it or a precise part of its activity. Select the
scale and coverage for that use, including a required whole-figure design view.

Derive visibility constraints from the depiction. Require coverage only where
the commission, adopted framing or intended effect needs it. A face, garment
or other identity feature can remain defined while partly covered by an object
or by marks on the image surface. Readability and recognition have scopes and
degrees; they do not reserve every component's entire contour. Use
[visibility and surface order](../finishing-layers-specification.md#visibility-and-surface-order)
to construct meaningful crossings. Apply
the same scope to negative text and review criteria. Terms such as `cropped`,
`out of frame` and `cropped feet` can counter an intentional composition; they
are not universal quality negatives. A crop, occlusion and broken construction
are different conditions. Assess the connections and design the selected view
implies, and keep only exclusions relevant to that view or an evidenced failure.
Do not expose the whole body merely to make completeness easy to check.

Revisit open framing when the purpose or scene changes. Retain a fixed view for
an authorized local edit, an adopted sequence grammar or another applicable
requirement. An earlier agent-selected frame remains a reusable candidate, not
a new user constraint. Repeated framing can serve a coherent series; variation
can serve distinct moments. Neither repetition, random rotation, a diversity
quota nor always choosing the opposite of the previous frame establishes good
direction. Judge each first proposal by its actual brief and retain successful
choices where their reasons still apply.

### Form a prompt from an image

Determine the reference's contribution from the request, the image and the
conversation together. Distinguish an example that establishes the desired
picture from one supplying a particular property to an otherwise settled
picture. For the former, carry its relevant whole construction through the
requested changes, including distinctive small choices, arrangement, action
and treatment. For the latter, an assigned contribution such as only its pose
or lighting stays narrow. The context decides this distinction; the user's
choice of words such as atmosphere, style or recreation does not decide it
alone. The author resolves that scope; a role label records the decision
rather than making it. Record the consequential interpretation in the existing
direction and reference uses, with its actual basis in the request and source.

Read informal preference language as evidence about the desired experience.
A person may point out the bright field, large words or sense of lightness
because those are the qualities they can name. Unless they limit the transfer,
that account is not an exhaustive list of the source relationships to use.
Inspect what makes those qualities work together, including unmentioned
spacing, contrast, boundaries and scale. For a whole-picture example, the
visible construction is part of the supplied brief. Develop the requested
changes within it; silence about a visible relationship does not assign that
relationship a negligible role. The author supplies the craft decisions needed
to realize the preference, including the contributions a nonexpert has no words
for. A source used only for a particular property does not authorize importing
its person, copy or belongings into an otherwise settled picture.

Distinguish freedom to choose content from freedom to discard its visual
function. Unspecified wording can leave the language open while the words'
large form, density, voice and crossings still organize the requested picture.
Open costume details can coexist with a selected distribution of light and
dark; a new character can coexist with a selected color treatment. Decide the
content and reconstruct its contribution together. An omission or substitution
needs the same whole-picture justification as an addition. When the conversation
actually selects atmosphere alone, other source choices can remain open. Ask
about a consequential unresolved choice only when the available evidence and
delegated scope cannot settle it.

Read the visible image before using an accompanying prompt to explain it.
Describe the encounter it offers: the viewer's distance, the dominant masses
and intervals, what feels still or moving, and what the eye can continue to
discover. Read how the depicted contents become that visible pattern: their
proportions, simplified or modeled planes, marks, joined tones and interrupted
boundaries. A detailed source can simplify its large forms and skin while
concentrating fine marks elsewhere. Naming its objects, palette and medium
leaves those choices open. Consult
[shape and mark](../finishing-layers-specification.md#shape-and-mark-as-one-drawing-grammar)
for the actual construction. Distinguish depicted space from the image surface.
A word can sit on that surface, carry a body's weight in an invented space, or
be painted on a wall; those are different pictures even when the copy and
character match.

Read the whole before selecting and excluding contributions. Follow the
relationships that make this particular image cohere. A curved machine can
counterbalance a slight body; a face and nearby hand can enclose an intimate
interval; a large quiet field can let a dense group thrust toward the viewer.
Look at the whole, the meaningful groups within it and the relevant local
construction. Identify what carries the first impression and what sustains
looking, including how different treatments meet. Keep the degree: a slightly
closer portrait is not an extreme crop, and a neat title beside a figure does
not reproduce letters that occupy most of the picture and interlock with the
body. Small material or graphic choices can be essential to that larger
effect. Their function determines whether they matter, rather than their size
or whether the user mentioned them.

For a close transfer, establish which connected choices make the source
recognizable as a construction and which can change under the commission.
Test the reading by considering the resulting picture if an important relation
were removed, weakened or replaced. An evenly spaced collection can retain
every object while losing the experience of being surrounded. A continuous
palette can retain every hue while removing the separation between a subject
and its field. A useful product diagram can retain the equipment while
changing what the viewer is invited to admire. A change that loses the selected
effect needs another realization or an acknowledged change of direction.
Meeting the request's spoken nouns and adjectives can still produce a different
picture from its supplied visual example. Assess the selected construction as
well as that verbal compatibility. Permission to choose a different word,
garment or object does not by itself establish that the resulting loss of
scale, color distribution, surface behavior or graphic force is acceptable.
Neither a technique count nor an inventory of every visible mark settles this.

Keep this source-based understanding available beside the draft that implements
it. The working note can be brief: the connected relations to retain, the
allowed substitutions and the resulting limits. Saved work uses its existing
direction and criteria. Compare the reconstruction with the original material
again after writing it, including relationships the draft left out or changed.
Read the source at its assigned scope and the complete input as the picture it
requests. A list of features selected from the draft is a narrower comparison
basis than the original source. The ordinary author performs this comparison;
independent evaluation is a separate task when that evidence is needed.

When substituting a subject, propagate the change through the construction.
Body shape changes contact and silhouette; a new word changes the available
letter shapes; different equipment changes the figure's balance and clearances.
Changed local colors or garments change the large value and color masses, even
when identity is perfectly retained. Use the craft in
[Visual construction](../finishing-layers-specification.md#large-form-interval-and-degree)
to reconstruct the chosen relationships through those consequences. Adjust
the choices still open instead of placing a new subject into unchanged
coordinates or reducing the whole to fit a convenient pose. A permitted change
can prevent exact correspondence; state that particular limit without treating
the rest of the construction as unconstrained.

Apply identity and treatment at their declared scopes through
[design and representation](render-contract.md#represent-the-selected-design).
A design source need not fix the rendering, pose or background. Conversely,
selecting a new treatment does not authorize a different person or object.
Resolve which source defines the subject, which defines its representation
here, and how required distinctions survive that representation. A specific
recognition detail need not force the rest of the picture into the source's
medium, palette treatment or inspection view.

When the reference arrives after a first result, interpret the new direction
before freezing the old picture's properties. Praise for an expression, object
or atmosphere retains that contribution, not every means used to produce it.
A request to replace the underlying construction can reopen arrangement and
treatment while retaining the liked meaning. A bounded local edit still
preserves its actual baseline. Select earlier results as model inputs for the
properties they still contribute, and state that scope. Use the existing
[semantic edit](semantic-instruction-edit.md#resolve-the-intended-change)
contract when the task actually edits an image.

Separate a visible property from an explanation of how it might have arisen.
A glass object can be recognized through its edges, transmitted colors and
reflections without the image establishing an exact lens or light rig. A
specific reflection matters when reproducing that composition; a glass
material concept can carry ordinary reflection behavior in a new arrangement.
Describe uncertain material or hidden construction through what is supported
instead of inventing manufacturing details, anatomy or an artist attribution.

When an actual prompt or reusable template is available, read it completely
after the image reading. Distinguish its target construction, input-dependent
choices, defaults and proposed production sequence. A template with unfilled
fields is not evidence of the inputs used for its sample. Visible words or
objects do not reveal those historical values. The text can demand a flatter
treatment, different crop or stronger omission than the sample exhibits.
Establish whether this commission follows the text, the visible sample or a
specified combination; do not silently average them. A described sequence of
rendering phases is not evidence that separate operations actually occurred.
Translate necessary final-state relationships into the selected target's input;
use only actual supported operations for any required production sequence.

#### Carry the selected image contribution into the input

Write the resulting depiction for the actual input. State the organizing
relationship and its degree, then the consequential realization and changes.
Carry the chosen drawing behavior through the content it governs: how the
face, fabric, machinery, light or type form the shared pattern. A general style
label does not settle relationships left open by otherwise detailed prose.
Use the target's supported vocabulary and relationship syntax to express that
same construction; the number of style words does not establish its transfer.
If the image is only an authoring source, express its necessary contribution in
text. If delivered, bind its selected contribution to the actual attachment and
state the relationships that the new subject, words or treatment must preserve.
A broad assignment such as atmosphere does not carry a separately selected
close construction. A supplied image can carry rich visible detail without
every mark being transcribed, but it does not settle conflicting prose or a
role that expressly discards a needed contribution. Use
[reference-to-prompt synthesis](../visual-reference-activation-and-transport.md#reference-to-prompt-synthesis)
to inspect that combined input, then compare its picture with the retained
source-based understanding under [Observe and repair](#observe-and-repair).
An already directed picture needs this comparison, not a new conception contest.

An inferred prompt is a candidate way to obtain an appearance. Pixels do not
reveal the original wording, conditioning or editing history. A feature absent
from known text may come from ordinary completion, another input or a later
change. Preserve that uncertainty, and keep the current input's responsibility
for the requested result distinct from claims about how the source was made.

### Form a prompt from an idea

Establish what the request asks to develop, the roles of its subjects and any
stated use. [Establish the character for this use](#establish-the-character-for-this-use)
as the character proposal and its picture develop together. A sparse
character brief still asks for a depiction of that character. Develop what
makes the character particular through the choices relevant to the request;
character or form design can itself be the creative question. Alternatives
may propose different character designs where those choices are open, with a
concrete definition in each. A scene, object, explanation or other commission
retains its own requested content and roles. Supplied and adopted material
continues to govern at its assigned scope. Missing detail gives room to develop
that work; an unstated use does not become an invented commission.

An informal request can establish an experience or practical use without naming
a visual technique. Understand what the person wants to recognize, feel, learn
or use, and which supplied facts, words or design properties must remain true.
Also distinguish an open invitation to invent from an attempt to recover an
unstated visual preference. The same short request can fit many substantially
different pictures. Its brevity supplies no evidence that the author prefers
a familiar scene, gentle mood, realistic proportions or explanatory layout.

Use the conversation to obtain consequential preference information when it is
needed. Ask what the person likes about something they have seen, what they
want to keep looking at, or what would make the result feel wrong to them.
Accept an image, a comparison or ordinary words such as "the person looks tiny
next to it" or "the writing and the person feel like one thing." The agent
translates that observation into construction; the requester need not diagnose
layout, perspective or typography. A question should admit answers that change
the proposal. Asking only for a color or slogan after settling the picture
does not discover its direction. Ask for a useful reference when the intended
likeness cannot be resolved from the available description; do not pretend to
infer a hidden image from a generic request.

Keep this exchange proportionate. Existing information and delegated creative
scope can already be enough to proceed. For an open commission, develop real
possibilities and make the chosen proposal concrete; do not require the user
to complete a design questionnaire or approve routine choices. If the user
is deciding between directions, describe or show the actual differences in
ordinary language. Treat their response as new preference evidence, not as
proof that the agent's prior interpretation was correct. Preserve the raw
request and the information acquired through the exchange in the existing
working records; do not rewrite the starting brief to include later discoveries.

Choose the consequential question within this scope. A narrative event, an
emotional premise or a graphic metaphor is one possible means, not a required
starting point. The proposed result needs a particular visual construction;
it need not make a separate thematic statement. The assistant's courteous
interaction with its user supplies no emotional or moral register for the work.

Determine which choices the prompt needs to settle and which can remain with
the image model. A complete prompt can specify the requested content without
selecting a medium, rendering style or incidental arrangement. Do not invent
those choices to complete a taxonomy or add a finish sentence to satisfy a
form. Preserve explicit and applicable adopted requirements; leaving other
properties open does not remove them. Assess whether the actual input is
sufficient for the requested work, rather than how many choices it fixes.

Develop the choices that matter by working on the actual proposed picture.
Use the following sequence as one author's ordinary production work. An already
directed picture or bounded local edit can move directly to its realization.
Keep working material at the existing owners: the conversation, source notes,
editable prompt and, for saved work, task direction. The amount of exploration
follows the unresolved choice and the consequence of getting it wrong.

1. **Read and investigate what could change the proposal.** Establish the
   character and its present situation at the needed depth. Consult
   [Creative core](../creative-core.md) and
   [Visual construction](../finishing-layers-specification.md) for open picture
   design, and investigate useful meanings through
   [Discovery](sparse-discovery.md) and the definition vocabulary procedure.
   Read selected records in full. Search by the intended effect and unresolved
   relationship as well as by a familiar technique. Distinguish supplied facts,
   source scope, learned craft and authored invention. Investigate enough to
   make the consequential choice; an unsuitable or empty result is useful
   information, and an authored expression can carry a meaning the dictionary
   lacks. Carry an exact selected model and adopted style into the work, using
   [rendering guidance](render-contract.md#read-the-models-guidance) and
   [model evidence](model-evidence.md#keep-kinds-of-evidence-apart) for their
   actual capabilities. Without a selected model, retain that realization limit.
2. **Construct the depiction at the scale of the desired effect.** Develop
   content, performance and presentation together. A request for greater force,
   intimacy, strangeness or clarity can concern how the whole work addresses
   its viewer: its organizing form, presentation, medium, surface, sequence or
   relationship between image and text, as well as a physical action or view.
   Consider those freedoms through their consequences for this particular
   character and request. A proposed presentation is a creative means; a user
   can choose a result without already knowing its technical name. When a real
   uncertainty requires comparison, write sufficiently concrete alternative
   constructions before selecting between them. For requested alternatives or
   broad reconsideration, use [actual candidate comparison](authoring-work.md#compare-actual-candidates)
   to read their complete inputs and shared source applications together.
   Identify a shared authored choice that narrows the emerging constructions.
   When that governing choice remains open, construct another actual resolution
   and follow its implications before selecting between the inputs. A shared
   planning note does not supply this comparison. Retain established requirements
   and choose the consequential question from this work rather than a fixed
   category or inventory of every open detail. When a direction already
   has an adequate basis, develop it directly. The evidence is the actual
   constructions and their fit, rather than a quota of alternatives or a list
   of style labels. Preserve the commissioned character, roles and stated use
   through any change in presentation.
3. **Write, compare and revise the actual input.** Carry the chosen character
   definition, expressive forms and organizing relationships into the prompt
   and selected references. Read those complete inputs as the picture they ask
   for, then compare that picture with the original request and applicable
   sources. Check what the viewer encounters about this character and whether
   the chosen means produce the intended effect at its required degree. A
   convincing rationale cannot supply a relationship missing from the input.
   Revise the responsible construction when it falls short; keep a sufficient
   one and continue. Present the useful proposal and its determining reason.
   An important unresolved preference can be offered as a concrete choice the
   user can understand. Routine choices remain with the author.

The agent chooses the amount of working detail. Its actual source consultation,
constructed proposal, revised input and result can be inspected; a statement
that the procedure was followed proves neither comparison nor artistic quality.
The same process works for one author in conversation or saved production.
An independent evaluator or user-role simulation belongs to a separately
requested review or maintenance study. Repetition can serve an adopted series,
a preference or a task; variation can serve a changed experience. Both are
judged through the commissioned result.

Develop content and visual construction together at the resolution this
commission needs. Make the choices that give the requested subject or design
its particular effect. Form itself can carry that work; a material process or
visual rhythm can inform its conception. A medium's name alone does not
resolve the treatment. Select consequential qualities rather than completing
an axis inventory or appending a reusable finish paragraph. Carry their actual
visual behavior into the prompt. Use
[visual treatment](render-contract.md#choose-the-visual-treatment)
for its construction and recording, within this same direction process.

Keep the requested subjects and their roles intact when developing the whole
construction. A setting can have substantial expressive force in a character
depiction while serving the character being developed. A place, object or
relationship can itself carry the work when the commission assigns it that
role. Resolve the construction and visibility needed for the requested result;
clarity for inspection alone does not establish what should matter most.

Give the intended experience a concrete visual proposal. An idea such as
"an inviting workshop" leaves useful choices: an open workbench prepared for
a visitor, people sharing tools, or a warmly occupied room seen from its
entrance. Choose a construction that serves the brief, then develop that
picture. Listing every familiar sign of invitation would mix different
solutions. A detailed brief may already select the construction and leave
only its rendition to be authored.

Use production knowledge during inquiry and candidate construction to connect
possible effects to suitable means.
An informal self-portrait relates the subject to the camera; an interrupted
activity relates attention to unfinished work; a cutaway reveals concealed
structure; transmitted light can make colored glass luminous; broad flat
shapes can make a poster legible at a glance. These are usable visual
relationships, not mandatory recipes. A cinematic scene can use deep focus
when an event in the background matters. A precious object can be presented
with bright quiet lighting or dramatic dark surroundings. Select the variant
whose consequences fit this picture.

Develop the selected construction through the qualities and relationships that
carry its effect. A form's proportions, a material's appearance, a participant's
response, the arrangement of a setting or the correspondence between pieces of
information can organize the whole picture. Give those choices their intended
degree and compose the surrounding parts so they sustain that reading. A
smoke-bodied creature needs recognizable form and dissolution to coexist; an
object advertisement needs its identifying design and material response to
work together; an explanatory image needs its comparison or mechanism to be
understood. Placement and presentation follow from what must be encountered
together. Familiar concepts can carry ordinary construction while distinctive
design, action or comparison receives the needed specificity. Detailed ornament,
unusual anatomy, an exact inscription or a particular hand-object action can
carry the whole commission. Keep that precision even when other parts remain
open.

Search by the effect or construction still open, as well as by a known term.
Knowledge can supply a useful concept the author did not yet know to name.
Read its meaning, scope and alternatives before closing the choice, then
express the selected picture in the target's language. A concept's familiar
associations help construction; they do not override an explicit design.
No particular image source or catalog match is required. Use adequate subject
and craft knowledge when the inspected records do not fit, retaining the
actual inquiry, constructions and comparison in ordinary working
notes rather than manufacturing a lookup or a new planning form.

### Worked depictions

These are independent synthetic examples of authoring choices, not templates.

**Appearance to language.** Suppose an authoring photograph shows an amber
glass perfume bottle, a ribbed stopper, a narrow cream label and an illuminated
amber patch on the table behind it. For a recreation, a suitable instruction
is:

> A studio product photograph of an amber glass perfume bottle with a ribbed
> stopper and a narrow cream label. Warm light passes through the bottle and
> casts an amber patch behind it on the pale tabletop. Keep the bottle's
> silhouette and label clear against the quiet background.

The material, transmitted light and their relationship describe the effect.
The distinctive stopper and label remain explicit. An exact recreation may
also need their shape, proportions and placement from the actual image. If
the commission borrows only the luminous-glass treatment for a different
product, the stopper and label do not transfer. Neither use establishes an
unseen lamp model or a numerical lighting angle.

**Idea to language.** A brief asks for a welcoming illustration of a guardian
made of living smoke. One direction is strength conveyed by a broad stable
form, with an open greeting and soft dissolution at its edges:

> A powerful guardian made of living smoke offers an open hand in welcome.
> Its broad central form remains recognizable while smoke curls away from
> the outer contours. A calm expression and an eye-level encounter make its
> strength reassuring. Use a richly colored fantasy illustration with clear
> large forms and soft atmospheric edges.

The material concept supplies possibilities for wisps and gradients. The
central form and greeting specify what those possibilities must support.
Individual wisps need no fixed paths unless their design is commissioned.
An ominous guardian would call for a different performance and presentation,
not simply more smoke detail. This wording is an authored possibility, not
evidence that an unspecified model will render it successfully.

**A relationship as the subject.** A brief asks for a diagram explaining how
two meshed gears reverse the direction of rotation. A suitable depiction shows
their engaging teeth and the opposite rotation arrows together. The contact
and corresponding arrows carry the explanation; merely making the larger gear
more prominent leaves the mechanism unexplained. In a flat diagram, proximity,
alignment and consistent symbols organize this relationship. It needs no
physical camera. Tooth count or precise dimensions remain explicit when those
are part of the requested explanation.

**Situation.** The request is a quietly comic kitchen illustration of an
absorbed cook missing milk boiling over. A possible prompt is:

> In a small home kitchen, a cook studies a recipe at the counter, absorbed in
> the page while milk foams over a saucepan on the stove beside them. The
> unnoticed overflow supplies the quiet comedy. Show the cook's reading and
> the overflowing saucepan together in a warm, unhurried domestic illustration.

The cook's attention and the unnoticed event make the picture. Their relation
selects the framing. The act of reading carries ordinary handling of the
recipe; no invented elbow angle is needed to make it concrete. A different
pose can preserve the same event. A close portrait that loses the saucepan
would lose the requested situation even if its expression were beautifully
specified. Additional kitchen or character detail can be appropriate for a
richer commission without replacing that relationship.

**Reviewing a proposed design image.** The request is a catalog photograph of
a cream ceramic cup with three thin cobalt rings near the rim and a plain,
undecorated handle, showing its exterior design clearly. Working notes also
identify the internally approved sample as D8. A submitted draft says:

> Make an extreme overhead close-up looking into the cream cup. Let its
> interior fill the image, with the handle behind it. Keep the three thin
> cobalt rings near the rim and the undecorated handle clearly visible.
> Put "Approved sample D8" in the upper-right corner. Use soft studio light.

The review concerns whether this is a good direction for the commissioned
catalog image. Obeying its overhead view and label does not answer that
question. The draft directs attention into the cup while the commission is
to present the exterior design. Adding another demand for visible rings and
handle leaves that organizing choice in place. The approval code belongs to
the working notes; the commission did not ask the renderer to display it.

Rebuild the product presentation and retain the actual design requirements.
The ring count, color, placement and plain handle need precision. The draft's
view and label have no independent authority. Select a slightly elevated
three-quarter view: it presents the exterior rings and handle together while
the visible rim makes the cup's form legible. A level frontal view can also
show the decoration; the three-quarter view better combines those requirements
in this catalog image. The replacement is:

> A clean catalog photograph of a cream glazed ceramic cup on a neutral
> background. Exactly three thin cobalt rings encircle the cup just below the
> rim; the handle is plain cream with no decoration. A slightly elevated
> three-quarter view shows the exterior pattern and the handle together.
> Soft studio light reveals the glaze without obscuring the cobalt lines.

This changes the presentation, preserves the exact design and carries only
relevant meaning from the working notes. It does not repair the draft by
adding an angle, a visibility checklist or a ban on internal sample labels.
The selected view is still a means: another view that presents this design
clearly can fulfill the commission. A generated image that faithfully follows
the overhead draft can satisfy that draft while failing the catalog purpose.
Reviewing only whether the angle, label and counts appear would miss that
failure. Reviewing only the broad purpose while dropping a required ring
would also fail the commission.

If only the draft were supplied for review, its stated catalog purpose and
exterior-design requirements would still support criticism of the overhead
presentation. Explain that inferred purpose and any authority uncertainty.
Do not assume an unseen approval, but do not require one in order to judge a
proposed instruction. These examples illustrate judgment; their chosen poses,
views, objects and amount of detail are not defaults for other tasks.

When the request spans related images, use the existing
[Project Design](../../templates/narrative/design/design-template.md) for shared
recognition anchors, each output's purpose and relationships between outputs.
Adapt treatment to each use while preserving declared continuity. Each separate
image has its own intelligible depiction, Production Specification and request;
a composed page is itself one selected output. The batch route executes those
prepared requests after their design is resolved.

## Record the production direction

The task's `direction` holds `purpose`, `intended_effect`, applied source IDs
in `basis`, `decisions`, `source_allowances`, `action_slice`, and `limitations`.
Use empty lists for decisions or allowances the work has not made, and `null`
for an inapplicable action slice. Each decision is a current adopted instruction
within this task, with its exact expression and realization.
Keep the distinction between an intended effect and an audience response
actually observed; a manifest or passing test proves neither.

Record criteria for the result the artifact must provide, at its actual scope.
Ground them in the commission, applicable adopted definitions, current state
and conditions necessary for the intended use. Carry those requirements forward
when a later request relies on them. Preserve baseline choices outside the
authorized scope of a local edit. Apply each requirement at its authored meaning,
precision and conditions, including fine details and meaningful absences.

Use `hard` for required outcomes and properties, and `advisory` for useful
preferences or optional improvements. Establish an inferred necessity through
its consequence for the requested result or use. Consider a coherent alternative
that changes the proposed condition while meeting the applicable requirements;
when it works, retain that freedom in the criterion. Chosen staging, observable
cues, quantities and production hypotheses belong to the realization. Judge
their success through the result they produce. A chosen means becomes fixed
through the scope of an actual requirement or adoption. Explicitly requested
details remain requirements regardless of their prominence.

Use the existing criterion `text` and `evidence` to describe the required result
and what would support, contradict or leave it undecided. Preserve permitted
variation and requested ambiguity. For an individualized performance, state the
concrete defining relationships and current selection at the precision its
source establishes, following [Performance Language](../performance-language-specification.md#9-evidence-for-a-performance-judgment).
Where the source requires a particular defining relationship, an emotion label
alone does not preserve that distinction. Direct emotion words remain valid
for the intended impression and can accompany its selected visible expression.
Do not turn every
incidental pose choice into a permanent trait, or treat a selected form as
interchangeable with another merely because both express the same emotion.
A visible depiction, supplied text, measured
structure and documented state support different judgments. Use
`direction.limitations` for evidence boundaries and keep wider source knowledge
available for comparison. Background facts need not all become depicted claims,
and a still cannot establish an unshown conversation or earlier choice.

Connect the required meaning or property to its carrier in the selected text,
controls or media. Assess the presentation by what that combination communicates
at useful scale. When a view excludes genuinely necessary information, change
the construction within authority; more wording cannot expose it. When another
combination carries the same result, keep that realization available. The
authority for a requirement, the selected way to realize it and the returned
evidence remain distinct. Consequential limits and unresolved authorial choices
stay at their existing owners rather than automatically becoming instructions
to the renderer.

Write the current construction directly in each decision's `expression` and
`realization.instructions`. When alternatives would resolve a consequential
choice or the user requests several, compose actual candidates and compare
their effects against the current commission. Each can carry a different
organization of the picture within the retained requirements. Select or present
the requested candidates through [Authoring work](authoring-work.md). Keep
unfinished work as a draft and develop it as the choice becomes clear.

Each decision names its `id`, applied source IDs in `basis`, `expression`,
`realization`, criterion IDs in `criteria`, predecessor decisions in
`depends_on`, and intentional `deviations`. An optional `note` explains the
current choice. A deviation records its source ID, source locator,
scope and reason, and leaves canonical identity unchanged. Detailed
personality, experience, physique, relationships and context may matter
together; read them together rather than looking up a stock gesture from an
emotion. Restraint, an unchanged stance, expressive exaggeration and a
deliberate contrast with ordinary behavior can all be valid choices.

A decision's `realization` describes the actual `method`, executable
`instructions`, an optional applied `capability_source` and known
`limitations`. Check the selected tool's real accepted controls; precise
language about a pose or motion leaves open whether a model can realize it.
Use generation, existing artwork, reference preparation, composition, local
edits or another supported operation according to the purpose; a longer prompt
is one repair among several.

Declare each hard criterion's actual `origin` and `observation` once in the
criterion. The compiler retains these choices with the captured request and
source bytes; no manual hash or repeated output-medium field is needed.

- `origin: {"kind": "user-request"}` cites the actual commission retained in
  the work basis, including an expressly accepted choice.
- `origin: {"kind": "adopted-source", "source": "SOURCE_ID"}` cites an applied
  source at its selected scope. Read its actual authority, conditions and
  permitted variation. A source ID alone does not establish adoption.
- `origin: {"kind": "agent-realization", "decision": "DECISION_ID"}` cites a
  current selected means and is permitted only for an advisory criterion.

Approval of a communicated concept covers the choices actually presented.
Later details added to a task-local definition remain authored realization
unless the request or an adopted source independently requires them. Keep that
distinction when a criterion refers to the definition as a whole.

The observation's `scope` states what the criterion evaluates. Use `observable`
for content directly supported by its `evidence`, `impression` for an apparent
emotion or mood, `sequence` for ordering, `duration` for elapsed time, or
`comparison` with `baseline: "SOURCE_ID"` for a comparison against an applied
source. `context-only`, `author-only` and `unresolved` retain their limitations
and cannot be hard result criteria. Optional `decision: "DECISION_ID"` names
the particular direction decision being assessed, not every decision that
helps achieve the goal. Preparation derives the medium from `artifact` and
checks it against the criterion's existing `evidence`.

For example, when a happy expression is actually requested:

```json
{
  "id": "happy-expression",
  "strength": "hard",
  "text": "The requested happy expression is readable.",
  "evidence": "image",
  "origin": {"kind": "user-request"},
  "observation": {"scope": "impression"}
}
```

An unrequested ear angle chosen to realize that happiness remains advisory.
Do not stamp every criterion as `user-request` to satisfy validation. The
validator follows declared authority and references; the author must compare
the criterion's actual meaning with its cited original.

An ordinary decision's executable instructions are its `visible` carrier.
Where another role matters, set `representation.role` on that decision:

| Role | Selected input and review scope |
| --- | --- |
| `visible` | Direct primary depiction, carried by the actual expression and executable instructions. This is the ordinary role when no representation override is needed. |
| `secondary-figurative` | A secondary impression, with `representation.primary` naming an independently resolved visible decision. Its assertion is an impression and its importance is secondary. Its literal content cannot be a hard criterion. |
| `context-only` | Necessary scene premise included for understanding. It is not hard proof of an unshown fact. |
| `author-only` | Retained privately in the direction; omitted from consumer directives. Keep it out of the separately authored prompt and media too. |
| `unresolved` | An undecided representation. Completing preparation waits for the author to resolve this selected decision. |

These declarations apply to selected decisions, not every word or every fact
in a source. Keep source-only material at its existing owner when no selected
direction is needed. A secondary impression may help a required happy
expression: bind the criterion to the primary expression when naming a
particular decision, and keep the figurative thought out of literal proof.

Image artifacts ordinarily use `single-still`. Set `direction.image_format`
to `multi-panel` for an intentionally ordered panel image. A hard `sequence`
criterion then uses image evidence; a single still cannot supply it. Video uses
motion evidence, audio uses audio evidence, and authored text uses text evidence
at its own scope. Text and JSON can explicitly state a narrative interval such
as "waited ten minutes"; text evidence establishes that statement, not a measured
playback duration. Measured playback duration requires motion or audio evidence.
Panels do not establish elapsed time by themselves. Assess a visible time label
as observable content when the label is the requirement. Sound requires actual
audio evidence, including an audio stream when the artifact is video. Use the existing
`action_slice` when a still needs a particular phase, and retain its unverified
before/after facts outside the visible requirement.

Preparation checks these declared relationships through
`scripts/production_expression.py`. It reports the exact criterion or decision
and a repair action. It neither scans emotion words and metaphors nor certifies
meaning, aesthetic quality or actual approval. Saved evidence remains unchanged;
new authoring resolves an unsupported requirement at its responsible source.

### Keep authoring context out of consumer instructions

The bounded consumer and Generation Package receive **the current expression
and method instructions**. Private source prose, working alternatives, notes
and deviation records stay in the author's work. Author the
model-facing instructions deliberately: whatever you copy into the selected
instructions reaches the consumer, sensitive content included.

## Preserve accepted variation at its declared scope

An author's allowed range for the depiction belongs in
`direction.source_allowances`. Each entry names its unique `id`, applied approval
`source`, source `locator`, `author`, `scope`, `allowance`, `limits`, and affected
task `criteria`. The approval source is an applied task source. The allowance
has its own identity and authority, independently of a particular staging choice.
The criteria's hard/advisory status remains in force.

Use task scope for an allowance limited to this work task. Synthetic entry:

```json
{
  "id": "natural-fabric-folds",
  "source": "author-direction",
  "locator": "wardrobe allowances",
  "author": "request owner",
  "scope": {"kind": "task", "task_id": "outfit-study"},
  "allowance": "Fabric folds may change with the declared arm pose.",
  "limits": ["Keep the adopted garment color and closure structure."],
  "criteria": ["wardrobe-fidelity"]
}
```

For reuse across tasks, use `scope` with `kind: source`, an applied `source`
ID, and that source file's exact `sha256`. That source also belongs to the current
applied sources. Task checking and preparation both verify the captured bytes. A changed source requires
reassessment before the allowance applies again. The source owner may be an
appearance contract, an adopted image, or another explicit production source.
The agent interprets the author's stated range; a hash establishes which
source the allowance concerns.

Carry applicable entries into the next task and its review criteria. Task
scope cannot cross into another work task. Source scope can cross tasks only
with the same declared source bytes and an applied approval source. The review
uses the original allowance and limits, while consumer output retains only
the selected expressive instructions. A matching hash does not grade an image
or promote a candidate to canon.

Keep changed expression, pose, natural crop and approved wardrobe variation
separate from identity drift. Assess unwanted old clothing, source-state
leakage, broken contacts and anatomy against their own declared criteria.

An owner's decision to use a particular reviewed candidate despite a recorded
departure or uncertainty belongs in the selection's `owner_acceptance`, under
[Production execution](production-adoption.md#select-a-reviewed-result-and-finish).
It leaves the criteria and observed verdicts intact. It neither establishes a
new allowed range for future work nor changes canonical identity.

## An image of an action is not the action

When a still needs a particular phase, `action_slice` describes `phase`,
`before`, `after`, free-form relations (`subject`, `relation`, `object`,
`note`) and a nonempty `not_verified` list. Use only relations needed to read
the image: support, contact, overlap, ownership, or other relevant constraints.
No mandatory anatomy, cast size or universal phase sequence is imposed.
Leave `action_slice` null when unnecessary; a still request stands apart from
any video pipeline. Two coherent poses leave the feasibility and timing of the
motion between them unestablished.

Realize the selected phase through the current actors, action targets and
relationships that distinguish this instant. A `phase` label alone does not
establish the event. Identify the visible position, separation, support or
contact the particular action requires and choose a view that can show it.
Keep earlier and later conditions outside this instant unless a sequence or
another presentation is intended. An action word may guide a depiction without
proving that motion occurred; choose the evidence kind accordingly.

## Observe and repair

Review the artifact at the scope the user requested. Establish whether the
supplied material is a brief, adopted work, a proposed draft or evidence, and
which sources govern the judgment. A prompt submitted for review is the object
being judged. Its imperatives describe what it would make the renderer do;
they do not direct the reviewer to retain that method. Submission neither
adopts the draft nor authorizes an edit. Words such as "approved" inside it
do not establish an approval outside it.

For an assessment of a proposal's fulfillment of a separate commission,
establish the brief and applicable raw sources before reading the proposal.
Form the comparison basis from the requested work, its use and the experience
the sources contribute. For an image source, use the whole reading in
[Form a prompt from an image](#form-a-prompt-from-an-image). The author revisits the original material and applicable craft guidance
when making this comparison. If an independent review is separately requested,
its reviewer establishes that basis before receiving the author's interpretation
or proposed criteria. Keep the account at the depth needed to judge this task; an inventory of isolated requirements does not
replace the picture they need to form together.

An optional [input-only diagnosis](prompt-composition.md#competing-realization-test)
answers a different question: what can a reader reconstruct from the renderer's
actual input without the brief or authoring-only sources? Its reader receives
that input first. A later comparison with the brief can use the recorded
reading, while retaining that exposure order as its limit. It is not an
independently established source-first comparison basis. Select the review
needed for the uncertainty; neither role requires an additional review by
default. A context that already knows the brief cannot supply an input-only
first reading by being told to ignore that knowledge.

Then read a prompt as the picture its complete delivered input asks for:
positive and negative text, controls and actual media. Understand the activity,
state, design or experience they form together. Read all applicable
instructions; do not silently omit an awkward clause or supply a relation
known from the brief but absent from that input. Distinguish what the renderer
receives from what the reviewer knows. For a returned image, observe the
artifact first under
[Production execution](production-review.md#observe-before-interpreting),
then establish the commission and source basis for comparison. Textual intent
is not evidence that the image rendered it.

Compare that picture with the commission. Explain what the viewer can
understand or experience and how the organizing choices produce it. This
comparison identifies a weakness, a sufficient solution or an uncertainty
worth resolving. If there is no separate brief, infer the intended depiction
from the candidate's complete content and stated purpose, retaining that
limit. Its instructions constrain an accurate reading of what it currently asks for.
Assess whether those instructions are suitable choices, not how to preserve
all of them. When no separate brief is available, the candidate's expressed
purpose, subject and intended relationships can support a recommendation;
identify the interpretation instead of inventing an earlier brief. Exact
design properties may be the commissioned content, while a view or coordinate
may be a proposed means. Decide from their role in the actual task, not their
length, forceful phrasing or presence in the candidate. An uncertain external
commitment qualifies a proposed change; it does not make every draft sentence
untouchable or prevent criticism of what the current input does.

Use the relationships in the depicted situation to judge it. A formally
possible collection of positions can still give a strained performance or
obscure the action it was chosen to show. Conversely, a natural depiction can
be sufficiently directed without specifying every position. Judge what the
participants are doing and responding to, or how the designed parts function
together, at the specificity needed for the request. An attractive alternative
alone establishes no defect. Quiet performance, complexity, deliberate
ambiguity and unfamiliar designs retain their intended range.

Investigate the responsible choice when it affects that judgment. Follow
interacting conditions through one arrangement, state and presentation rather
than assigning each a separate favorable interpretation. Consider their
practical and expressive consequences, not only whether the wording can be
made logically consistent. An impossibility claim requires incompatible
conditions in the same scope. A workable construction rebuts that claim but
still needs to serve the commission. A consequential choice supplied by the
reviewer is a proposed completion, not proof the input already resolved it.
Ordinary variation inside the brief needs no exhaustive specification.

Assess local differences through their effect on the commissioned result and
applicable source requirements, at the meaning and precision those sources
establish. Accept a different realization that preserves those requirements
and their permitted variation. For an individualized expression, compare the
actual form with the original definition and current selection, not just the
emotion label or an incomplete prompt. An appealing different form can be a
new design proposal; its appeal does not supply a retrospective allowance.
For a real shortfall, choose a repair at the scope of its consequence and retain
the successful parts. Keep an optional improvement in its advisory role.
Conclude the investigation when that question is answered; neighboring details
do not become unfinished work merely because they are available to inspect.
Use the [competing-realization test](prompt-composition.md#competing-realization-test)
only for a consequential uncertainty about what the input communicates. It is
not a required second review of every prompt.

For an authorized revision, rebuild at the scope of the responsible choice.
Recover the intended situation or design from the request and sources, decide
what realization serves it, and author the changed result. Feedback about the
whole effect reopens the organizing choices that could produce that effect,
including presentation and medium where those remain open. A successful scene
with a local defect calls for a repair within that scene. Judge the consequence
first, then choose the operation.

Use [Authoring work](authoring-work.md) to perform that choice on the actual
candidate. An amendment retains the chosen baseline and applies the scoped
changes, including their necessary consequences. Composition constructs a
candidate from the current request and sources, using previous candidates as
comparison where useful. Choose between those operations by the relationships
the request preserves and reopens, rather than by the name of the property being
changed. The current request, applicable identity and adopted requirements remain
available to both operations. The agent resolves open creative choices and asks
only for a decision that the available authority cannot settle.

Use the [Revision Contract](revision-contract.md) to check a completed change
against the actual candidate and its authority. Saved work continues through
[Production authoring](production-authoring.md#keep-branches-and-reopen-completed-work).
Preserve the actual user request separately when a registered work goal is its
summary. Carry the selected result into the actual prompt, controls and source
applications before assessing the requested effect.

When feedback rejects the basis or focus of a review, reconsider that basis.
Recover the whole task and withdraw the unsupported premise and its dependent
recommendations. Correcting a local defect and correcting the question being
asked are different operations. Feedback on reasoning does not itself
commission a different image.

Read the completed rendition as the same commissioned picture with the change
applied. Preserve successful meaning, explicit detail and permitted variation.
Resolve a fixed requirement that the current construction cannot meet; ask
only for the necessary decision if authority cannot settle it. A prompt review
concludes sufficiency, recommends a selected improvement or identifies a
concrete blocker. State that conclusion first, with the consequence that
determines it. When the work meets the requirements, recommend its use and
complete the task. Optional improvements remain optional. For a needed repair,
describe the desired replacement and carry the selected change into the
existing revision workflow.

Keep conclusions within their evidence. Distinguish an observed image failure,
a textual weakness, an optional improvement and a model-specific hypothesis.
A prompt may direct an effect without proving rendered success or fidelity to
an unseen source. Unexamined qualities remain `not-assessed`; inspected but
inconclusive evidence is `indeterminate`. Neither is a pass or proof of a defect.
Assess hard criteria using their appropriate evidence kind: `artifact`, `text`,
`image`, `motion` or `audio`. Selection requires supported passes or the owner's
explicit acceptance of the particular assessed departures and uncertainties.
An unassessed hard criterion still needs review. Acceptance records a decision
to use the candidate; it supplies no missing observation or passing verdict.
A sufficient textual review can complete a text-only request without generation.

For recorded repairs, retain the observation, affected decision IDs, operation,
changed targets and reason in the existing review. `revision-intent` compares
the complete revised preparation with its parent and refuses changes beyond
reviewed targets. It binds the new input to an `edit` authorization and creates
a linked run through `revise`; previous successful checks and selection do not
transfer. Preserve candidates and evidence. State and source drift follow
[State Write-back and Drift Routing](../state-writeback-routing.md).
[Production review](production-review.md#review-repair-and-a-new-run) owns these commands and evidence;
[Production permissions](production-permissions.md) owns independent authority.

## A failure must leave an actionable record

For a recorded failed check, provide a concrete repair with its observation and
target links, or state the unresolved issue. Apply the check's justified
hard/advisory strength. Record incidental uncertainty in observations and
limitations, and retain optional improvements as proposed repairs while required
work completes. Use `unresolved` for a question whose resolution or explicit
owner acceptance is necessary to decide selection against the applicable
requirements. Keep an accepted issue and its actual assessment in the review.

A proposed repair is not an execution commitment or an observation of success.
An authorized pixel operation uses the actual image-edit executor; a change to
production inputs uses a reviewed child run. The result of either performed
repair requires its own review and selection before completion.
