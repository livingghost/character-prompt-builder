# Character Domains

Domain classification keeps anatomy, behavior, expression, material language, locomotion, and contact appropriate after the art direction has been chosen. It is not the creative core and does not decide the image's appeal.

Each canonical subject domain has a baseline `domain-realization` record in the active catalog. The schema value `realization_level: "foundation"` names that record-level tier; it is not a pack type or distribution boundary.

## Human

Useful production channels include facial structure, eyes, brows, mouth, jaw, head angle, hair mass, skin, hands, posture, clothing construction, gait, reach, and social gesture.

Baseline domain realization:

```text
domain-realization-human-baseline
```

## Anthropomorphic animal

Useful channels include species head construction, muzzle or beak, ears or head appendages, humanoid torso and limbs, hands or paws, lower-leg design, tail, fur or species surface, markings, clothing adaptation, and coordinated animal-human performance.

Baseline domain realization:

```text
domain-realization-anthropomorphic-animal-baseline
```

## Ordinary animal

Preserve natural anatomy and behavior. Let gaze, head direction, ear or feather position, body tension, breathing, locomotion, timing, distance, and environment carry emotion. Do not impose human pose or clothing by default.

Baseline domain realization:

```text
domain-realization-animal-baseline
```

## Creature

Establish one coherent body plan, sensory cluster, silhouette, appendage logic, locomotion, center of gravity, surface system, scale, and environmental contact before ornament.

Baseline domain realization:

```text
domain-realization-creature-baseline
```

## Hybrid

Choose a primary structural plan and deliberately place secondary traits with readable transitions, joints, support, material boundaries, and repeated motifs. The result should read as one identity.

Baseline domain realization:

```text
domain-realization-hybrid-baseline
```

## Robot or android

Design chassis or body architecture, articulation, panel hierarchy, optics or sensors, manipulators, materials, emissive elements, wear, tools, locomotion, and mechanical clearance as one functional system.

Baseline domain realization:

```text
domain-realization-robot-baseline
```

## Coordinated performance language

Every domain can express emotion and intent, but it does so through different visible channels. Read `performance-language-specification.md` before reducing a performance to an emotion label. Record the gaze target, facial or sensory state, posture, hands or manipulators, feet or locomotion, distance, contact, physiology or machine response, appendages, and timing that support the selected read.

One cue can support several meanings. Blush, tongue display, crossed arms, tail flicks, optic pulses, or head tilts gain meaning from the full channel pattern and relationship context. Keep alternate readings when the evidence remains ambiguous.

## Real, fictional, ordinary, and anthropomorphic subjects

Reality status does not replace domain classification. A real species name can be used in either an ordinary-animal or anthropomorphic design, while a fictional species can use creature, hybrid, anthropomorphic-animal, or another appropriate domain. The domain owns body plan, locomotion, behavior, contact, and clothing adaptation.

A natural tiger and an upright tiger person may share tiger species vocabulary, but they must use different domain realizations and different identity contracts. Generic ordinary animals normally need species, surface, behavior, environment, and scene records rather than a character archetype. An ordinary-animal archetype is appropriate only for a recurring individual with stable identity evidence such as a stripe map, ear notch, scar, unusual proportions, or persistent coloration. See `archetype-governance.md`.

## Multiple domains

A single image may contain several domains. Apply one realization to each materially different subject domain while preserving one image intent, art direction, medium, composition, and lighting system.

## Sparse wording

Interpret the whole brief using ordinary language. Ask only when different body forms are genuinely equally plausible and would materially change the image. Do not let a species keyword or preset decide the form in isolation.

Age appearance and gender presentation are ordinary creative descriptors. They have no hidden default and do not route a subject into different permitted scenes or styles.
