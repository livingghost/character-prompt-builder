# Reference-Corpus Visual-Technique Observation Guide

Use this guide when supplied images or prompt-output pairs inform new or revised
preset records. It complements `reference-corpus-detail-observation.md`, which
handles localized identity details. The observation knowledge below covers
whole-image drawing technique, proportion, camera, gaze, light, depth and
staging. Consult the applicable parts and `performance-language-specification.md`
for a performance question; they provide knowledge rather than fields to fill
for every source.

## Core rule

Understand the complete depiction before naming a style, scene or module.
Observe its relationships and presentation, then assign reusable knowledge to
its production function. Do not use subject matter as a shortcut for style or
treat every visible feature as a stable character trait. An explanation of the
image's construction is not automatically a list of necessary prompt clauses.

A useful observation must let another agent understand the visual decision without seeing the source image. Record relationships, not only labels.

## Evidence discipline

- Describe visible image-space facts before interpreting intent.
- Use relative measurements against local landmarks, not pixels or canvas coordinates.
- Distinguish a repeated system from a one-off accident, decoration, or state.
- Mark inferred information as inferred, especially when eyes, contacts, or support points are occluded.
- Compare against existing canonical records before creating a new ID.
- Remove source names, signatures, logos, readable text, franchise identifiers, exact insignia geometry, and obvious editorial censorship devices from reusable knowledge. A mosaic, blur patch, opaque paint shape, sticker, bar, warning label, or replacement mark is source contamination; preserve the underlying supported semantic category and keep hidden fine structure unspecified.

## Learn from a prompt-output pair

Keep three contributions distinct: what the source text asks for, what the
image visibly realizes, and what production knowledge may transfer to a new
task. Read the image before using the prompt to explain it. An independent
image reading can help expose assumptions when that comparison is the study's
question; it is not a new production gate.

Examine how the text selects meaningful concepts, relationships and distinctive
details. A material term can invoke edge, highlight and deformation behavior
together; a recognizable activity can imply ordinary handling and posture.
The useful lesson may be that connected concept rather than a separate
instruction for every resulting feature. Conversely, a polished output can
change the requested action or design. Record such departures without treating
the image as proof that its source wording was fully obeyed.

Use available generation context at its actual scope. A sidecar may contain
only the main prompt while embedded metadata records additional local prompts,
references, adapters or later image processing. Distinguish saved settings from
execution evidence and active connections from unused workflow nodes. Missing
context limits claims about wording effectiveness; it need not prevent visual
analysis. Model-specific syntax and response observations belong to their
model or dialect owner, not to a universal depiction rule.

One pair establishes a used input and an observed result when that association
is supported. It does not establish the necessity, isolated effect or optimality
of each token. Compare repeated inputs, variants and materially different uses
when available. If several conditions change, retain a comparison of complete
setups. Use a controlled trial only when the question actually concerns the
effect of a particular change.

Promote knowledge that helps choose and construct a new picture. State the
effect, the meaningful concept or relationship that can produce it, the
conditions where it is useful, and the choices that can vary. Keep precise
detail when it defines the construction. Distinguish a general concept from
one selected variant: a shallow-focus film still can be a reusable treatment,
but shallow focus is not an invariant of all cinematic images. Store source
pairs and their observations with their owning content collection; public
teaching examples use independent subjects and briefs.

## Observation knowledge

Use these questions where they explain the image or the reusable decision.
An image without a physical camera needs no inferred lens; a material study
needs no invented expression. Preserve consequential fine information and
uncertainty. The resulting record should teach a coherent construction rather
than reproduce this section's headings as a checklist.

### 1. Image intent and center of appeal

State what makes the image worth seeing and what relationship holds it together. Examples include monumental athletic confidence, viewer-close warmth, formal self-possession, or playful summer spectacle.

This is not yet a preset classification. It is the whole-image reading that prevents unrelated details from being averaged together.

### 2. Composition, depth, and focal hierarchy

Record:

- dominant masses and the negative spaces between them
- foreground, subject, and background layers
- crop boundaries and which body parts leave the frame
- overlap order
- focal sequence and supporting reads where the image establishes them
- how background value, edge density, and detail support the subject

A scene record may preserve these relationships. A style family may preserve only the recurring background and detail treatment, not the specific body arrangement.

### 3. Camera geometry and perspective

Record:

- camera distance: extreme close, close, medium, three-quarter, or full-body
- camera height relative to the subject, such as floor, waist, chest, eye, or overhead level
- pitch, yaw, and roll
- perspective strength and likely lens behavior
- which form is nearest to the lens
- near-to-far scale change
- horizon or waterline position when visible
- crop and frame clearance around ears, hands, feet, props, and effects

Separate base anatomy from perspective. A fist, knee, ball, muzzle, or paw enlarged because it is nearest to a wide-angle camera is not evidence that the character permanently has an oversized part. A proportion record stores the stable ratio; a camera, lens, composition, or scene record stores perspective enlargement.

### 4. Body proportion and shape hierarchy

Measure relative relationships such as:

- head width against shoulder span
- head height against chest depth
- hand or paw size against forearm width
- upper arm against head width
- ribcage against pelvis
- shoulder, chest, waist, and thigh mass
- neck thickness and the continuity from head to torso
- species-defining structures against the body silhouette

State which masses are structurally large and which only appear large through perspective. Preserve joint continuity and a readable head or sensory cluster even when the design intentionally contrasts a compact head with a broad body.

### 5. Head direction, eye direction, viewer relationship, and performance channels

Record these separately:

- head turn and pitch
- visible pupil direction
- gaze target
- eyelid and brow state
- muzzle, mouth, beak, jaw, or faceplate performance
- ear, horn, crest, antenna, or sensor direction
- shoulder and torso orientation toward or away from the target
- mouth, tongue, jaw, cheek flush, tears, breath, hands, feet, distance, and contact
- ears, tail, whiskers, ruff, wings, crest, horns, antennae, tentacles, and other domain-specific appendages
- optics, sensors, gimbals, manipulators, actuators, panels, indicators, ventilation, and timing for robots

When the eyes are hidden by a brim, visor, hair, shadow, or crop, do not claim a visible eye direction. Record the occlusion, then describe the viewer relationship carried by head axis, nose or muzzle direction, mouth corners, jaw, ears, and posture. This may justify a performance touch, but it does not create an eye-gaze fact that is not visible. Record each visible channel before naming the emotion. Keep alternate readings when the combination remains ambiguous.

### 6. Light geometry

Record the physical arrangement of light independently from the drawing technique:

- key direction, height, size, and color
- fill direction and relative strength
- rim or edge sources
- bounce from water, walls, floor, sky, fabric, or nearby objects
- cast-shadow direction
- environmental emitters and practical lights
- whether the face is intentionally protected from shadow collapse

This information belongs to lighting modules or scene staging when it is reusable.

### 7. Rendered value, shadow, and highlight grammar

Record how the image depicts light:

- number and size of value groups
- hard, soft, broken, or mixed shadow edges
- shadow shape design and whether it follows form terminators
- highlight count, size, edge quality, and curvature behavior
- flat patches versus gradients
- reserved bright glints
- ambient-occlusion or overlap accents
- whether blush, atmosphere, and sheen use different edge classes

Light geometry and rendered light are different. A warm source on the right is a lighting choice. One hard cel-shadow family plus one broad soft-falloff sheen per volume is a style-family choice. Do not store both as one vague phrase such as dramatic lighting.

### 8. Line and mark-making system

Record:

- outer-to-inner line-weight hierarchy
- taper behavior
- contour continuity
- interior-line density
- edge accents at overlaps and contact points
- treatment of fur, hair, scales, feathers, cloth folds, panels, or water contours
- whether the line system is clean, rough, washed, painterly, printed, or absent

A concrete style family requires line behavior to operate coherently with form, value, highlight, color, surface, background, and detail hierarchy.

### 9. Surface and material behavior

Record each visible material separately:

- local-color grouping
- matte, satin, glossy, wet, translucent, metallic, woven, smooth, furry, scaled, or plated response
- highlight width and edge behavior by material
- texture scale and density
- moisture, sweat, water beads, scratches, folds, seams, or wear
- contact shadows and compression

Temporary wetness, sweat, blush, dirt, injury, and lighting color are scene-visible state. Visual repetition alone cannot promote them into stable identity; only an explicit approved Identity, Era, Form, or Appearance Contract may assign a durable owner. A style family defines how a surface is drawn; material and state records define what the surface is.

### 10. Support, contact, and action chains

For the action or construction being learned, follow its relevant connected
structures and distinguish visible connections from inferred ones:

- shoulder to elbow to wrist to hand or paw
- hip to knee to ankle to foot
- manipulator to prop
- body to furniture, ground, water, or another subject
- garment strap to fastener to panel
- necklace, chain, cord, or tool attachment

Record support and weight, not only the named pose. Occluded roots or endpoints must remain inferable from the composition, or the staging should be revised before it becomes a curated scene.

### 11. Stable identity, adaptable design, and temporary state

Sort observed information into:

- stable identity: morphology, palette, markings, proportions, recurring distinctive details
- adaptable design: species, palette, outfit color, handedness, environment, expression intensity
- scene state: sweat, blush, wetness, damage, fatigue, weather response, current clothing condition
- source-specific content: signatures, logos, text, exact insignia, copyrighted character identifiers, and editorial censorship overlays

Only the first three can become reusable production knowledge, and each belongs to a different record family. Source-specific content is excluded.

Discard clearly non-diegetic censor masks, pasted blocks, mosaics, blur patches, replacement shapes, logos, signatures, UI remnants, and meaningless text before building the observation ledger. Their presence is not a scene fact and does not become an occlusion or concealment field. Continue the observation ledger from the depicted content and the selected subject contract.

## Classification after observation

Use the completed inventory to choose the smallest correct knowledge family.

| Observed recurring system | Catalog destination |
|---|---|
| Cross-domain appeal, viewer relationship, proportion emphasis, color-light taste, and finish hierarchy | universal aesthetic core |
| Line, form, shadow, highlight, color, surface, background, and detail acting as one indivisible finish | concrete style family |
| Anatomy, body plan, species performance, locomotion, manipulation, surface translation, and environmental interaction for one domain | domain realization or domain-specific aesthetic touch |
| Physical light-source arrangement independent of medium | lighting module or scene lighting geometry |
| Stable relative body-part scale | proportion or body-build module |
| Camera distance, height, pitch, lens behavior, crop, and perspective strength | camera, lens, composition, or scene |
| Head direction, eye target, or performance under occlusion | head-gaze, expression, or aesthetic-touch module |
| Reusable meaning carried by several coordinated visible channels | body-language-cue module |
| Reusable support, contact, action, depth, crop, and focal order | base scene |
| One garment, accessory, gesture, contact, or local construction solution | atomic module |
| Stable localized identity feature | distinctive-detail module |
| One predictable failure and its positive repair | correction |

Do not create a style family from a shared species, muscular build, low camera, glossy skin state, or one lighting setup alone. Do not create a scene when the reusable knowledge is only a cap, apron, hand gesture, camera treatment, or proportion rule.

## Cross-scene family audit

When several supplied images appear related, first remove their shared subject matter and stage facts. Compare only the drawing system:

- outer and interior line hierarchy
- shape construction and mass connection
- value grouping and shadow edge behavior
- highlight count, width, edge, and placement
- local-color stability and accent flow
- material and surface abstraction
- background density and separation grammar
- focal and detail hierarchy

Require at least three materially different situations and agreement on at least six of these eight axes before proposing a new or generalized family. Record which scene attributes were removed, identify the nearest existing family, and state at least two visible boundary rules. Then perform a cross-application check by placing the grammar on a different scene and subject domain. If the result needs the original beach, bed, ruined city, garment, typography, pose, or effect to remain recognizable, the evidence belongs to a scene or module rather than a family.

When the evidence generalizes an existing scene-named family, revise the single canonical family and update every affected reference. Do not keep duplicate records as synonyms. When evidence is suggestive but incomplete, record a deferred candidate and the exact additional evidence needed.

## Promotion threshold

A vocabulary record may be promoted to curated when the reference material supplies a reusable visual function, a complete affirmative construction, at least three adaptation invariants, and at least three nearby misreadings. A new scene preserves the applicable body or object geometry, action, gaze, contact, depth, crop, focal hierarchy and lighting relationships, with adaptation fields and diagnostic failures. An invariant belongs to that construction's declared scope; a visible choice does not become invariant merely because it is easy to measure.

A new style family requires repeated evidence that the finish axes operate together under the cross-scene audit above. One strong image can justify an atomic module or scene when its construction is independently reusable, but one image rarely establishes a complete cross-domain style family by itself.

## Discovery handoff

After classification and authoring, convert the observation into user-facing discovery evidence. Do this after the visual relationships are understood so that search wording does not replace observation.

Record or generate:

- an `outcome_summary` describing what visible image the record can help produce
- at least three natural aliases that omit the exact ID and label
- scoped facets for species, body build, proportion, palette, camera, lighting, wardrobe, environment, mood, style, or other actual evidence
- a `discovery_group` and `variant_of` relationship when similar records exist
- an anchor signature for stable identity facts that recommendation must preserve
- one or more short ordinary-language regression queries when the record creates a new discovery path

Aliases should describe the image as a user might: `low camera chest dominant portrait`, `warm apron worker looking down`, or `ball near lens athletic action`. Do not use copied source titles, character names, logos, signatures, jersey text, exact insignia, or internal ID fragments as the only discovery language.

Keep modifier ownership explicit. A blue coat belongs to a subject-palette facet; blue rim light belongs to lighting; a blue wall belongs to environment; a glossy sheen drawing grammar belongs to style. This separation allows a sparse brief to preserve identity while exploring different scenes and treatments.

Read `references/runtime/sparse-discovery.md` for knowledge discovery and `references/maintenance/search-discovery.md` for index, grouping and evaluation requirements.

## Style-family boundary review

After the observation inventory, compare the extracted line, form, shadow, highlight, color, surface, background, and detail hierarchy against every nearby canonical family. Test the grammar on a materially different scene and remove scene nouns, species, body build, camera, relationship, temporary state, and source-specific graphics. If the finish survives, it may support a family. If new evidence shows that an existing family is named after a season, room, prop, or one effect even though the grammar transfers elsewhere, generalize that family through reviewed lossless authoring and update all references to its current canonical ID. Record the comparison in the family record's `nearest_family` and `boundary` fields. Validate a released pack and its required dependencies with `scripts/pack_release_gate.py`. Use `scripts/style_family_audit.py` only as a focused development diagnostic with the same explicit pack runtime.

## Record review

Review the completed record against its function and applicable evidence:

- The whole-image center of appeal was identified before parts were classified.
- Base proportion was separated from perspective enlargement.
- Camera distance, height, pitch, crop, and nearest form were recorded.
- Head direction and eye direction were not conflated.
- Light geometry was separated from shadow and highlight rendering grammar.
- Surface material was separated from temporary state.
- Support and contact chains are reconstructible.
- The record contains only knowledge belonging to its family.
- Existing records were inspected for overlap.
- A family proposal uses at least three cross-scene evidence items, removes scene facts, states its nearest-family boundary, and passes a cross-application check.
- Every family reference resolves directly to one current canonical record.
- Source text, logos, signatures, exact insignia, and character identity were removed.
- Positive fields describe the target affirmatively; misreadings and failures remain diagnostic.
- The record can be found through natural outcome language without its exact label, ID, or category name.
- Search facets preserve modifier ownership and do not confuse subject color, eye color, clothing, environment, lighting, and style.
- Near-duplicate records share a discovery group and expose their variants without crowding the result set.
## Distance and crop are visual technique

Camera distance and crop are part of the composition grammar. Record them with subject-relative measurements and frame landmarks, not only words such as `close` or `portrait`. Separate body size from perspective enlargement by naming the nearest form and the expected near-to-far scale change.

Garment geometry also participates in the visual read. Open armholes, narrow straps, low waistbands, high leg openings, and support-induced ride-up change the negative space and silhouette. Preserve those paths as measured construction rather than relying on the garment category name.
