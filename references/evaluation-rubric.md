# Creative Evaluation Rubric

Automated tests can verify package structure, retrieval contracts, data roles, and exact payload transport. They cannot prove that a prompt or image is artistically successful.

Evaluate skill-assisted work against a no-skill baseline and, where useful, alternate retrieval strategies.

## 1. Intent fidelity

- Does the result preserve the user's explicit requirements?
- Does it understand the whole phrase rather than translating fragments literally?
- Are gaze, action, relationship, and framing the intended concepts?
- Does it avoid replacing the brief with a nearby preset narrative?

## 2. Image intent

- Is there one clear reason the image is worth seeing?
- Do composition, performance, setting, and finish serve that reason?
- Does the result feel designed rather than merely complete?

## 3. Art-direction coherence

- Is the center of appeal identifiable?
- Is the subject's relationship to the viewer, another subject, or the environment clear?
- Do composition, shape rhythm, surface, color-light logic, and detail hierarchy belong to one direction?
- Is one medium family chosen deliberately?

## 4. Universal-core use

When a universal aesthetic core is selected:

- Does it strengthen the chosen direction rather than replace it?
- Does it remain cross-domain and free of irrelevant anatomy assumptions?
- Is at most one complete core used?
- Would the direction still be understandable without naming the core?

## 5. Domain realization

- Does the subject read correctly as human, anthropomorphic animal, ordinary animal, creature, hybrid, or robot?
- Are anatomy, body plan, locomotion, expressive channels, surfaces, manipulation, and contact appropriate to that domain?
- Does an ordinary animal remain naturally animal rather than acquiring human gesture by default?
- Does a robot express through optics, articulation, stance, and material systems rather than borrowed fur or human-skin language?
- For multiple subject domains, are their realizations coordinated coherently?

## 6. Medium fidelity

- Does the result clearly belong to the chosen medium?
- Has anime remained anime, photography remained photography, and 3D remained 3D?
- Are medium mechanics coherent across line, values, surfaces, light, background, and detail?
- Is mixed media present only as a deliberate direction?

## 7. Performance and relationship

- Do gaze, lids, brows, mouth, tongue, cheek color, physiology, head angle, body orientation, hands, feet, gesture, distance, contact, appendages, and timing tell the same story?
- Are felt emotion, displayed emotion, and masked or conflicted emotion separated when the scene needs that distinction?
- Does the viewer or partner relationship match the brief?
- Are alternate plausible interpretations retained until scene and relationship context select one?
- For anthropomorphic and animal subjects, do ears, tail, whiskers, ruff, wings, crest, and other appendages reinforce the selected read through species-valid behavior?
- For robots, do optics, sensors, manipulators, chassis posture, panels, indicators, actuators, ventilation, and timing express the state through functional mechanical changes?
- Are interactions supported by visible geometry rather than labels alone?

## 8. Composition and construction

- Is the silhouette readable?
- Are depth, support, weight, and crop coherent?
- Are hands, paws, manipulators, mouths, props, furniture, clothing, casing, and effects constructed clearly?
- Does foreshortening serve the image rather than damage readability?

## 9. Surface, color, and light

- Are relevant materials distinct and medium-appropriate?
- Does the lighting have one thesis?
- Do local colors and identity cues survive the light?
- Is surface detail concentrated where it helps form, identity, and contact?

## 10. Creative value

- Did the skill contribute useful visual decisions the user did not have to specify?
- Do invented details strengthen the image intent?
- Is the result more compelling and specific than a direct expansion of the input?
- Would an evaluator prefer to use this result?

## 11. Preset contribution

- Did curated records provide real production knowledge?
- Did vocabulary records remain lexical aids?
- Did retrieval broaden useful choices without introducing unrelated content?
- Did the final prompt integrate knowledge rather than concatenate records?

## 12. Negative-prompt discipline

- Are negative sources technically relevant and scoped?
- Are diagnostic alternatives kept out of the prompt unless activated?
- Are semantic exclusions present only when requested?
- Does the negative strategy match the target model's transport mechanism?

## Blind comparison

For meaningful evaluation:

1. use briefs not authored to match a specific preset
2. keep the image intent and target model constant
3. generate prompts under anonymous conditions
4. generate images with comparable model settings
5. randomize presentation order
6. judge prompt and image separately
7. record preference and reasons

Suggested conditions:

```text
A. no preset retrieval
B. broad atomic retrieval plus layered core/realization/profile candidates
C. curated-only atomic retrieval plus the same layered candidates
```

Retrieval counts are diagnostic. Final prompt and image quality determine value.

## 10. Distinctive identity detail

- Are localized features attached to the correct subject-relative side and landmark?
- Are count, size, shape, orientation, color relationship, depth, edge, age, and visibility consistent?
- Do signature details remain stable across camera, pose, outfit, and lighting changes?
- Did a localized feature become a vague global style cue or migrate to another body region?
