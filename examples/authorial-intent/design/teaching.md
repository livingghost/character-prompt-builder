# Scoped portrayal examples

These are independent hypothetical design probes, not one mandatory cast or a prescribed genre.
No proposed status here represents an actual author's approval. The examples demonstrate record
ownership and review; they do not prove artistic effectiveness.

## Authorial intent register

### Intent i-observation

- **status**: proposed
- **basis**: Hypothetical teaching design, not a user-approved project or evidence about a real person.
- **subject_scope**: An unpeopled setting in a three-part visual observation; no individual agent or internal emotion is specified.
- **portrayal_aim**: Let changing visibility make the same place feel different without changing its spatial relationships or supplying a final explanation of that feeling.
- **recognition_anchors**: The same opening, ledge and approach remain in their declared relation when visible. The organizing center is spatial, not a personality.
- **variation_envelope**: Light, occlusion and framing may vary. Do not relocate a landmark to create a mood; propose a separate world change if movement is desired.
- **contrast_and_cadence**: Return to the opening across the selected images while allowing its visibility to change; no prescribed mood ladder, climax or number of future returns.
- **departure_policy**: A relocation is a world/portrayal decision, not a lighting variation. An intentionally impossible cut requires a scoped author decision and audience treatment.
- **world_and_subject_dependencies**: Use only the conditions stated in this example. Specific embodiment, biography and world rules remain separate design choices.
- **priority_and_conflicts**: No implicit override. A conflicting adopted world condition or request requires an explicit scope decision.
- **information_boundary**: Author-facing design. Supply only applicable present instructions to a consumer; do not include planned outcomes.
- **realization_links**: notes/landscape.md contains an illustrative three-moment review, not completed generated images.
- **review_basis**: Review the proposed moments for landmark relations and open interpretation. Actual visual fidelity and audience effect have not been tested.

### Intent i-steadiness

- **status**: proposed
- **basis**: Hypothetical teaching design, not a user-approved project or evidence about a real person.
- **subject_scope**: One independent hypothetical speaking subject, in an ordinary task and a consequential interruption; current portrayal only.
- **portrayal_aim**: Make assurance legible through continuity of presence rather than through suddenly becoming serious.
- **recognition_anchors**: Economical complete responses, unhurried acceptance of attention, and action that does not retreat from the task. These are available choices, not mandatory gestures.
- **variation_envelope**: Information content, urgency and volume may change with circumstances. Extra visible fumbling is not added merely to prove emotional depth.
- **contrast_and_cadence**: Ordinary and consequential moments support the same bearing. The important moment gains weight from what is undertaken, not necessarily a new register.
- **departure_policy**: A collapse of bearing is possible only as a new scoped decision with its purpose, retained dimensions and aftermath; it is not automatically licensed by a strong emotion.
- **world_and_subject_dependencies**: Use only the conditions stated in this example. Specific embodiment, biography and world rules remain separate design choices.
- **priority_and_conflicts**: No implicit override. A conflicting adopted world condition or request requires an explicit scope decision.
- **information_boundary**: Author-facing design. Supply only applicable present instructions to a consumer; do not include planned outcomes.
- **realization_links**: notes/steady.md contains a provisional short sequence.
- **review_basis**: Compare the ordinary, interrupted and aftermath moments. Their plausibility alone is not proof of the intended accumulated identity.

### Intent i-switch

- **status**: proposed
- **basis**: Hypothetical teaching design, not a user-approved project or evidence about a real person.
- **subject_scope**: A separate hypothetical speaking-subject design, not a required counterpart to i-steadiness; present task context only.
- **portrayal_aim**: Let casual accessibility and focused responsibility both be recognizable, with the chosen transition carrying the promise of support.
- **recognition_anchors**: Ordinary conversational invitation and a context-bound move to a clear commitment. Neither is declared a mask or a truer self.
- **variation_envelope**: Informal address may remain while joking and digressions stop. Recovery can be gradual. Seriousness in every moment would remove the intended contrast.
- **contrast_and_cadence**: Give the usual ease actual room and use focused assurance where the stated purpose calls for it. Do not impose a fixed proportion or a switch in every scene.
- **departure_policy**: The planned mode switch is in range, not a rupture. Abandoning the commitment or losing the ability to switch needs a separately scoped decision.
- **world_and_subject_dependencies**: Use only the conditions stated in this example. Specific embodiment, biography and world rules remain separate design choices.
- **priority_and_conflicts**: No implicit override. A conflicting adopted world condition or request requires an explicit scope decision.
- **information_boundary**: Author-facing design. Supply only applicable present instructions to a consumer; do not include planned outcomes.
- **realization_links**: notes/switch.md contains a provisional short sequence.
- **review_basis**: Inspect onset, the actual commitment and the later return without assuming an instant emotional reset. No completed work or audience study is claimed.
